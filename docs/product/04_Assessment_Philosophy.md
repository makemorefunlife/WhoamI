## 1. Purpose

This document defines how every Ahaitsme assessment must behave.

It is the canonical owner of Ahaitsme’s:

* assessment philosophy,
* evidence philosophy,
* confidence philosophy,
* assessment boundaries,
* and constitutional assessment principles.

Ahaitsme assessments exist to help people understand themselves, other people, relationships, situations, and choices more clearly.

They do not exist to replace human judgment, define identity, predict destiny, or create dependence on the product.

# 3. Constitutional Principles

## Law 1. Assessment Strengthens Human Judgment

> **Assessment exists to strengthen human judgment, never to replace it.**

Ahaitsme may clarify patterns, tensions, possibilities, and tradeoffs.

It must not make consequential personal decisions on behalf of the user.

An assessment may support judgment.

It must not claim authority over the user’s life.

### Implications

* The user remains the final decision-maker.
* Assessment should expand understanding rather than prescribe obedience.
* Advice must not be presented as an unquestionable answer.
* The product must not imply that its conclusion is more authoritative than lived experience.

---

## Law 2. Assessment Reveals Patterns, Not People

> **Assessment reveals patterns, tendencies, and relationships. It must never define identity, assign worth, or reduce a person to a label.**

A pattern is not a permanent identity.

A type is not a rank.

A difference is not a defect.

Assessment language must distinguish between:

* what appears in the available evidence,
* what may be situational,
* and who a person fundamentally is.

### Implications

Assessment must not say or imply:

* that a person is inherently good or bad,
* that one type is superior to another,
* that a person cannot change,
* or that one result fully explains a human being.

---

## Law 3. Evidence Precedes Interpretation

> **Every interpretation must remain grounded in evidence. Claims must never exceed what the available evidence can support.**

The required order is:

```text
Evidence
↓
Assessment
↓
Interpretation
↓
Reflection
```

The order must never become:

```text
Narrative
↓
Assumed conclusion
↓
Retrofitted evidence
```

Narrative may explain a supported finding.

It may not create one.

### Implications

* Unsupported claims must not enter the assessment.
* AI-generated language must remain downstream of validated evidence.
* Interpretation must preserve the meaning and limits of its source.
* Multiple signals should be preferred when a conclusion requires multiple forms of support.
* A compelling story is never a substitute for evidence.

---

## Law 4. Uncertainty Is Better Than False Certainty

> **When evidence is insufficient, uncertainty, omission, or a request for more evidence is more truthful than fabricated certainty.**

Ahaitsme must be willing to say:

* unknown,
* inconclusive,
* not enough evidence,
* partially supported,
* or not assessed.

The product must never fill an evidence gap merely because a complete answer would feel more satisfying.

### Implications

* Missing evidence must remain missing.
* Ambiguity must not be disguised as precision.
* Weak signals must not be upgraded into firm conclusions.
* Omission is preferable to invention.
* The system should preserve unresolved states when resolution is not justified.

---

## Law 5. Confidence Changes Expression, Never Truth

> **Confidence determines how strongly a finding is expressed. It never creates facts, evidence, or conclusions.**

Confidence is a property of support.

It is not truth itself.

The following concepts must remain distinct:

```text
Confidence ≠ Truth
Confidence ≠ Severity
Confidence ≠ Quality
Confidence ≠ Importance
```

A high-confidence result may be stated more directly.

A low-confidence result must be expressed more cautiously or omitted.

Neither condition permits the system to change the underlying finding.

### Implications

Confidence may affect:

* wording,
* prominence,
* emphasis,
* qualification,
* and whether a result is shown.

Confidence may not:

* create evidence,
* reverse a conclusion,
* exaggerate significance,
* or convert possibility into fact.

---

## Law 6. Assessment Describes Possibilities, Never Destinies

> **Assessment may describe tendencies, conditions, and possibilities. It never predicts predetermined outcomes.**

Ahaitsme does not claim to know what must happen.

It may describe:

* recurring tendencies,
* likely friction under certain conditions,
* areas of resilience,
* possible consequences,
* or factors that may influence an outcome.

Every future-facing interpretation must remain conditional.

### Implications

Assessment must not claim:

* that a relationship will succeed or fail,
* that a person will inevitably behave in one way,
* that a career outcome is predetermined,
* or that a present pattern guarantees a future result.

The correct form is conditional:

> This pattern may create friction if it remains unchanged.

Not deterministic:

> This relationship will fail.

---

## Law 7. Understanding Precedes Action

> **Action has value only when it grows from understanding. Not every assessment must end with advice.**

Ahaitsme does not begin with a recommendation and work backward toward justification.

The proper sequence is:

```text
Understanding
↓
Reflection
↓
Choice
↓
Possible action
```

Advice without understanding is instruction, not insight.

Some assessments may appropriately end with:

* recognition,
* reflection,
* a better question,
* or a clearer conversation.

### Implications

* Advice must be traceable to the assessment.
* Recommendations must not appear before the user understands the underlying pattern.
* Reflection is a valid outcome.
* Action should remain proportional to the evidence.
* The product must not force every insight into a checklist or prescription.

---

## Law 8. Internal Validity Does Not Require User Visibility

> **Not every valid internal computation belongs in the user experience. Only what supports understanding should become visible.**

Ahaitsme may use many internal calculations, labels, thresholds, signals, confidence states, and intermediate judgments.

Their existence does not automatically justify exposing them to the user.

The product should:

```text
Compute what is necessary.
Reveal what is useful.
```

### Implications

The following may remain internal when disclosure would not improve understanding:

* raw scores,
* technical labels,
* confidence calculations,
* provenance mechanics,
* intermediate states,
* implementation details,
* and internal quality controls.

User-facing information should be selected based on meaning, not merely availability.

Internal complexity must not become external cognitive burden.

---

## Law 9. Assessment Preserves Dignity and Independence

> **A successful assessment preserves human dignity and leaves people with greater clarity, not greater dependence.**

Assessment must describe behavior and patterns without assigning moral worth.

It must not:

* shame,
* blame,
* stereotype,
* humiliate,
* romanticize harm,
* excuse abuse,
* or make the user feel powerless without the product.

A successful result should increase:

* clarity,
* self-awareness,
* agency,
* understanding,
* and confidence in human judgment.

It should not increase:

* fear,
* confusion,
* fatalism,
* emotional dependency,
* or reliance on repeated assessment for permission to act.

# 4. Evidence Philosophy

## 4.1 Evidence Is the Boundary of the Claim

Every claim must remain inside the limits of its supporting evidence.

The assessment may interpret evidence.

It may not enlarge it.

---

## 4.2 Evidence Sources Must Retain Their Meaning

Different evidence sources may answer different questions.

No source should be presented as proving something it was not designed or capable of establishing.

Evidence may include, depending on the product:

* user-provided facts,
* structured assessment responses,
* behavioral or situational input,
* psychometric signals,
* relationship comparison signals,
* domain-specific analytical frameworks,
* Saju-derived signals,
* or validated combinations of multiple sources.

The presence of a source does not automatically make it appropriate for every claim.

---

## 4.3 Multi-Signal Support Is Preferred for Complex Claims

The more consequential or complex the claim, the stronger and more varied its supporting evidence should be.

A single signal may support a narrow observation.

It should not silently become a complete explanation of a person, relationship, or outcome.

---

## 4.4 Contradictory Evidence Must Remain Visible Internally

When evidence sources disagree, the system must not erase the disagreement merely to produce a cleaner narrative.

Contradiction may result in:

* a qualified finding,
* reduced confidence,
* multiple possible interpretations,
* a request for more evidence,
* or omission.

The system must not select whichever source produces the most dramatic story.

---

## 4.5 User Input Does Not Automatically Override Other Evidence

User-provided answers are meaningful evidence.

They are not automatically the sole truth.

Likewise, analytical frameworks must not automatically override the user’s lived experience.

When meaningful sources conflict, the assessment should:

* identify the conflict,
* distinguish observation from interpretation,
* and preserve uncertainty where needed.

---

# 5. Assessment Boundaries

Ahaitsme assessment is not:

* a diagnosis,
* a moral judgment,
* a legal determination,
* a medical conclusion,
* a guaranteed prediction,
* a substitute for professional care,
* or an authority over the user’s identity and decisions.

Assessment must not:

* fabricate missing evidence,
* present unsupported certainty,
* classify human worth,
* rank people as superior or inferior,
* treat difference as failure,
* predict unavoidable outcomes,
* conceal meaningful uncertainty,
* or manipulate the user into continued dependence.

Assessment may:

* reveal patterns,
* explain differences,
* surface tensions,
* identify possible strengths,
* clarify tradeoffs,
* support reflection,
* improve conversations,
* and help users make more informed choices.

## Ownership Rule

A lower-level document may translate a constitutional principle into an applied rule.

It may not restate itself as an independent owner of that principle.

For example:

```text
Assessment Philosophy
Low confidence must not be expressed as certainty.
```

```text
Narrative Bible
Defines the approved wording patterns for low-confidence findings.
```

The first owns the principle.

The second owns its narrative application.

# 7. Governance

Changes to this document require review at the product-constitution level.

A change should be accepted only when it:

* applies across current and future assessment domains,
* remains valid regardless of implementation technology,
* does not belong more appropriately in a lower-level document,
* and is expected to remain true over the long term.

Implementation details, current thresholds, UI patterns, scoring ranges, model names, and temporary product rules must not be promoted into this document.

This document should remain small, stable, and authoritative.

---

# Final Constitutional Summary

Every Ahaitsme assessment must:

1. strengthen human judgment,
2. reveal patterns without defining people,
3. keep interpretation grounded in evidence,
4. prefer uncertainty over false certainty,
5. use confidence only to control expression,
6. describe possibilities without predicting destiny,
7. place understanding before action,
8. reveal only what supports user understanding,
9. and preserve human dignity and independence.
