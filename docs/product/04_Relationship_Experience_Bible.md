## 1. Purpose

Ahaitsme does not exist to deliver more relationship information.

It exists to help people recognize themselves, understand another person, make sense of a shared pattern, and see what they can do next.

A Relationship Report is therefore not a collection of sections. It is a designed realization journey.

Its success is measured by whether the reader naturally arrives at:

> "아, 그래서 내가 그랬구나."  
> "그래서 상대는 그렇게 느꼈구나."  
> "그래서 우리는 이런 흐름을 만들었구나."  
> "그러면 앞으로는 이렇게 해볼 수 있겠네."

The product does not merely explain relationships.

It designs the moment of **“AHA! It’s ME.”**

## 2. Authority and Scope

This document is the parent Experience SSOT shared by all Relationship Products.

It governs:

- the reader’s emotional progression;
- the order in which understanding is built;
- the role of each experience stage;
- information depth and pacing;
- the relationship between story, evidence, visualization, and action;
- the experience rules inherited by every Product Blueprint.

It does not govern:

- relationship calculation or analytical truth;
- product-specific content inventory;
- exact copy, tone, humor, or address rules;
- component, layout, color, typography, or motion;
- prompt construction;
- technical architecture or implementation.

When another document conflicts with this document on experience order or reader journey, this document governs unless an explicit product exception has been approved and documented.

## 3. Core Experience Philosophy

### 3.1 The Product Is Discovery, Not Delivery

A report should not feel like the system is announcing conclusions.

It should feel like the reader is gradually discovering something that becomes increasingly clear.

Insights must feel earned through progression, not dropped as isolated claims.

### 3.2 The Reader Is the Main Character

The relationship is not the protagonist.

The other person is not the protagonist.

The protagonist is the reader’s expanding understanding.

Every report must move through:

```text
Understand myself
↓
Understand the other person
↓
Understand our interaction
↓
Recognize choice and possibility
```

Even when a report analyzes two people equally, the experience remains anchored in what the reader can recognize, understand, and act on.

### 3.3 Relationship Insight Must Return to Self-Understanding

Relationship analysis is valuable because relationships reveal the self.

The report may explain compatibility, conflict, roles, rhythm, environment, or shared potential, but it must ultimately help the reader see:

- what they need;
- how they interpret behavior;
- what they repeatedly contribute;
- what they misunderstand;
- what they can choose differently.

The relationship is the lens. Self-understanding is the lasting value.

### 3.4 The Report Is One Continuous Story

Visible “Part 1,” “Part 2,” and “Part 3” structures should not define the user experience.

Internal modules may remain necessary for planning and implementation. The reader, however, should experience one connected journey in which each stage answers the question created by the stage before it.

The report should feel discovered, not assembled.

## 4. Canonical Experience Principles

### 4.1 Recognition Before Explanation

Recognition always precedes explanation.

The reader first needs to feel, “맞아, 이런 적이 있었어.” Only then will deeper reasoning feel relevant and trustworthy.

### 4.2 Understanding Before Advice

Advice delivered too early feels generic.

The report must first establish what is happening, why it happens, and how both people experience it. Action becomes meaningful only after understanding has been earned.

### 4.3 Meaning Before Detail

A score, axis, signal, or trait has no experience value until the reader understands what it means in real life.

The report should introduce the human meaning first, then provide the detail needed to support it.

### 4.4 Story Before Inventory

The report must not expose every available insight simply because the system can produce it.

Content belongs only when it advances the reader’s realization journey.

A smaller number of connected insights is more valuable than a large inventory of unrelated truths.

### 4.5 Evidence Without Interruption

Evidence must support trust without breaking immersion.

Saju signals, psychological axes, comparison logic, and confidence indicators should strengthen an insight, not force the reader to decode the engine.

### 4.6 Curiosity Through Progressive Disclosure

Each stage should resolve one question while naturally creating the next.

The reader should move forward because understanding is unfolding, not because more cards remain below.

### 4.7 Difference Without Judgment

Difference is not automatically conflict, deficiency, or incompatibility.

The experience must distinguish among:

- neutral difference;
- complementary difference;
- recurring friction;
- accumulated strain;
- structural risk.

The report must not escalate emotional severity beyond the supporting evidence.

### 4.8 Complexity Without Confusion

The engine may be complex. The experience must not be.

The report should preserve nuance while presenting one clear realization at a time.

### 4.9 Hope Without False Reassurance

Every report should leave room for agency and growth.

Hope must come from a clearer understanding of choices, not from minimizing genuine difficulty.

### 4.10 Action Must Be Earned

Every recommendation must trace back to an insight already established in the report.

No generic advice. No disconnected tips. No action section that could belong to any pair.

## 5. Universal Emotional Arc

All Relationship Products share the same emotional arc.

Their subject matter changes. Their experience logic does not.

```text
Recognition
↓
Curiosity
↓
Understanding
↓
Discovery
↓
Perspective
↓
Integration
↓
Agency
↓
Hope
```

### 5.1 Recognition — “맞네.”

The reader immediately sees something familiar and specific.

The opening should create emotional credibility before analytical depth.

### 5.2 Curiosity — “왜 그렇지?”

Recognition opens a question.

The reader wants to know why the pattern exists and whether the report can explain it accurately.

### 5.3 Understanding — “그래서 그랬구나.”

The report connects behavior to underlying needs, tendencies, or interpretation patterns.

What felt random begins to make sense.

### 5.4 Discovery — “이건 몰랐네.”

The reader encounters a meaningful insight that is not merely a restatement of what they already know.

This is the primary “AHA” moment.

### 5.5 Perspective — “상대도 그럴 수 있겠구나.”

The report expands beyond the reader’s viewpoint without invalidating it.

The other person becomes more understandable, not simply categorized.

### 5.6 Integration — “그래서 우리가 이렇게 되는구나.”

Separate traits and reactions are connected into a shared interaction pattern.

The relationship becomes legible as a system rather than a list of two personalities.

### 5.7 Agency — “우리가 바꿀 수 있는 지점이 있네.”

The reader sees where choice is possible.

The report identifies leverage points rather than demanding complete personality change.

### 5.8 Hope — “앞으로는 다르게 해볼 수 있겠다.”

The experience ends with grounded possibility.

The final emotion should be clarity and willingness, not judgment or fear.

## 6. Canonical Experience Architecture

Every Relationship Product inherits the following architecture.

Product Blueprints may rename or combine stages, but they must preserve each stage’s experience job.

### 6.1 Opening: Emotional Entry

**Job:** Create immediate relevance and invite the reader into the report.

The opening should:

- establish the relationship’s central emotional truth;
- feel personal without overstating certainty;
- create recognition before presenting structure;
- give the reader a reason to continue.

The opening should not:

- summarize the entire report;
- begin with methodology;
- overwhelm the reader with scores;
- define the relationship with a final verdict.

### 6.2 Self Recognition: “How I Enter This Relationship”

**Job:** Help the reader recognize their own needs, expectations, and default responses.

This stage anchors the report in self-understanding. It should explain the reader’s internal logic before evaluating the relationship.

### 6.3 Other-Person Recognition: “How They May Experience It”

**Job:** Introduce the other person as a coherent human perspective.

This is not a second personality report. It includes only the traits and needs required to understand the shared dynamic.

### 6.4 Shared Pattern Discovery: “What Happens Between Us”

**Job:** Show how two tendencies combine into a recurring interaction.

This stage converts “me” and “you” into “us.” It should reveal a pattern neither individual profile can explain alone.

### 6.5 Meaning and Evidence: “Why This Pattern Exists”

**Job:** Deepen trust by connecting the pattern to its strongest supporting signals.

Evidence should clarify:

- what drives the pattern;
- which differences matter;
- which similarities reinforce it;
- how confident the interpretation is;
- where the reading remains tentative.

### 6.6 Strength and Potential: “What Works Because of This”

**Job:** Reveal the value already present in the relationship.

Strength must not be decorative praise. It should emerge from the same pattern that creates difficulty, showing the full shape of the dynamic.

### 6.7 Friction and Risk: “Where the Pattern Can Hurt”

**Job:** Name the cost of the pattern without turning it into a verdict.

The experience should distinguish manageable friction from deeper strain and explain the conditions under which difficulty grows.

### 6.8 Growth Leverage: “What Changes the Pattern”

**Job:** Identify the smallest meaningful shifts with the greatest relational effect.

Growth should focus on translation, timing, environment, boundaries, repair, or role adjustment—not personality replacement.

### 6.9 Practical Action: “What We Can Try”

**Job:** Convert insight into behavior.

Actions should be:

- specific;
- feasible;
- connected to earlier findings;
- proportionate to the severity of the issue;
- useful in a real situation.

### 6.10 Closing Reflection: “What I Understand Now”

**Job:** Integrate the experience and leave the reader with grounded hope.

The closing should not introduce major new analysis. It should help the reader carry forward one clear understanding and one possible next step.

## 7. Story and Reading Flow

### 7.1 One Realization at a Time

Each stage should have one primary job.

A section may contain multiple signals, but it should produce one dominant realization.

### 7.2 Every Stage Must Connect

A stage should either:

- answer a question created earlier;
- deepen a previous realization;
- reveal a consequence;
- prepare the reader for action.

If it does none of these, it does not belong in the canonical flow.

### 7.3 The Middle Holds the Greatest Depth

The experience should begin lightly, deepen in the middle, and become lighter again near the end.

```text
Accessible opening
↓
Increasing depth
↓
Deepest interaction insight
↓
Practical simplification
↓
Reflective close
```

The reader should not experience continuous cognitive escalation.

### 7.4 Transitions Carry the Story

Transitions must communicate why the next idea matters.

A report should not move from one topic to another merely because the content inventory requires it.

### 7.5 Repetition Must Add Meaning

A core pattern may appear more than once only when its function changes.

For example:

- first as recognition;
- later as causal explanation;
- later as an action target.

Repeating the same conclusion in different words is not progression.

## 8. Experience Layers

A complete Relationship Experience uses several layers. No single layer should dominate the report.

### 8.1 Emotional Layer

Creates recognition, trust, curiosity, relief, and hope.

### 8.2 Narrative Layer

Connects separate insights into one understandable journey.

### 8.3 Analytical Layer

Provides the reasoning, comparisons, and distinctions that make the interpretation credible.

### 8.4 Evidence Layer

Shows the strongest supporting signals and communicates confidence honestly.

### 8.5 Visual Layer

Reduces cognitive effort and makes meaningful comparison easier.

### 8.6 Action Layer

Turns understanding into realistic behavior.

These layers should operate together. A report fails when it is emotionally engaging but analytically weak, analytically rich but exhausting, visually polished but empty, or actionable without sufficient understanding.

---

## 9. Role of the 11 Psychological Axes

The 11 axes are evidence infrastructure, not the experience itself.

They exist to:

- clarify important differences and similarities;
- support relationship-specific interpretations;
- distinguish surface behavior from underlying tendencies;
- strengthen or refine saju-derived readings;
- provide comparable structure across products.

They should not:

- become a mandatory 11-item tour;
- force equal attention to every axis;
- appear simply because data exists;
- replace product-specific relationship questions;
- turn the report into a technical dashboard.

Each Product Blueprint should select only the axes that materially advance its story.

The reader should remember the realization, not the axis inventory.

## 10. Visualization Philosophy

Visualization is a tool of understanding, not decoration and not proof by appearance.

A visualization must perform at least one clear job:

1. make a pattern easier to understand;
2. make two people easier to compare;
3. reveal change, distance, balance, direction, or interaction;
4. make an important insight easier to remember.

A visualization should not exist when prose communicates the point more clearly.

### 10.1 Meaning Before Graphic

The reader should know what question a visual answers.

A graph without a clear interpretive purpose creates work rather than clarity.

### 10.2 Selective, Not Exhaustive

Not every measurable trait deserves a chart.

Visual priority belongs to patterns that are difficult to grasp through language alone or central enough to anchor the report.

### 10.3 Comparison Must Be Fair

Visual scales, labels, and ranges must not exaggerate difference or imply precision unsupported by the engine.

### 10.4 Visuals Need Interpretation

A chart does not replace insight.

Every important visual should be paired with a concise explanation of what matters and why.

### 10.5 No Decorative Complexity

Complex visuals are justified only when they simplify a complex idea.

A visual that requires prolonged decoding has failed its experience role.

## 11. Information Density and Pacing

### 11.1 Depth Must Be Earned

The report should not begin at maximum complexity.

The reader first needs relevance, then explanation, then evidence.

### 11.2 One Primary Insight Per Unit

A screen, section, or narrative unit should have one dominant message.

Supporting details may deepen it, but they should not compete with it.

### 11.3 More Data Is Not More Value

Additional content is valuable only when it improves recognition, understanding, differentiation, confidence, or action.

### 11.4 Emotional Weight Must Be Managed

Difficult findings should not be stacked without relief, context, strength, or agency.

The experience should create space between heavy insights and avoid making the relationship feel more hopeless than the evidence supports.

### 11.5 The Ending Must Become Simpler

After the report reaches its deepest insight, the experience should narrow toward a few practical priorities.

The reader should finish clearer than when they began, not burdened by everything they now know.

## 12. Confidence, Severity, and Emotional Safety

Experience confidence and relationship severity are separate dimensions.

### 12.1 Confidence

Confidence describes how strongly the available evidence supports an interpretation.

Low-confidence insights must be framed as possibilities, not conclusions.

### 12.2 Severity

Severity describes the likely relational impact of a pattern.

A useful canonical ladder is:

```text
S0 — Strength
S1 — Difference
S2 — Friction
S3 — Strain
S4 — Structural Risk
```

Products may adapt labels, but they must preserve the distinction between ordinary difference and serious risk.

### 12.3 No Diagnostic Authority

The report must not present tendencies as diagnoses, destiny, moral judgment, or certainty about another person’s intent.

### 12.4 No Fear-Based Retention

The experience must never exaggerate threat to increase engagement, conversion, or perceived depth.

### 12.5 Difficult Truth Requires Agency

When the report names a serious pattern, it must also clarify:

- what the pattern does and does not mean;
- which conditions intensify it;
- what can realistically change;
- when boundaries, support, or outside help may matter.

## 13. Product Adaptation

Every Relationship Product inherits the same experience arc while answering a different central question.

### 13.1 Romantic

**Central question:** How do we love, seek closeness, and repair differently?

The experience emphasizes attraction, emotional needs, reassurance, conflict, intimacy, roles, and recovery.

### 13.2 Marriage/Cohabitation

**Central question:** How do we build and sustain a shared life?

The experience emphasizes daily systems, money, responsibilities, environment, decision-making, endurance, and long-term adaptation.

Marriage and cohabitation may share one product family when their experience question is the same: not merely how two people love, but how they live together in reality.

### 13.3 Family

**Central question:** Why can people shaped by the same family experience it so differently?

The experience emphasizes roles, recognition, guidance, emotional climate, distance, home environment, repeated family patterns, and intergenerational growth.

### 13.4 Friend

**Central question:** What makes this friendship feel easy, meaningful, distant, or difficult?

The experience emphasizes mutuality, social energy, loyalty, expectations, communication rhythm, recognition, boundaries, and repair.

### 13.5 Work

**Central question:** How do we create better work together?

The experience emphasizes contribution, decision-making, feedback, pace, structure, recognition, tension, reliability, and shared execution.

### 13.6 Same Arc, Different Content

Products must not redesign the emotional journey from zero.

They may vary:

- entry question;
- selected evidence;
- visual forms;
- interaction patterns;
- action types;
- narrative register.

They must preserve:

- recognition before explanation;
- self before shared pattern;
- understanding before advice;
- evidence before severity;
- agency before ending.

The goal is consistency of experience, not uniformity of content.

## 14. Inheritance Rules for Product Blueprints

Every Product Blueprint must explicitly map its content to this Experience Bible.

At minimum, each Blueprint must define:

1. its central reader question;
2. its opening recognition moment;
3. how self-understanding is established;
4. how the other person is made understandable;
5. its primary shared-pattern discoveries;
6. which evidence and axes are necessary;
7. how strengths and difficulties are balanced;
8. its highest-value growth levers;
9. its practical action format;
10. its closing reflection.

A Product Blueprint may depart from the canonical order only when:

- the product’s relationship context makes the default sequence harmful or confusing;
- the alternative still preserves the emotional arc;
- the exception is documented with rationale;
- the exception does not create an unnecessary standalone UX system.

## 15. Canonical Experience Validation

A Relationship Product is not experience-ready until it passes the following review.

### 15.1 Recognition

- Does the opening feel immediately relevant?
- Can the reader recognize themselves before encountering technical detail?
- Is the recognition specific enough to feel earned?

### 15.2 Progression

- Does each stage answer or deepen the stage before it?
- Is there a clear emotional and logical arc?
- Does the report feel like one story rather than stacked modules?

### 15.3 Discovery

- Does the report produce at least one genuine new realization?
- Is the discovery more than a paraphrase of visible traits?
- Does it explain interaction rather than merely compare two people?

### 15.4 Evidence

- Are major claims supported by relevant evidence?
- Is confidence communicated honestly?
- Are the 11 axes used selectively rather than mechanically?

### 15.5 Density

- Is every included insight necessary?
- Are repeated ideas performing different jobs?
- Does complexity peak in the middle and reduce toward the end?

### 15.6 Visualization

- Does every major visual answer a clear question?
- Does it simplify rather than decorate?
- Is the scale fair and the interpretation explicit?

### 15.7 Emotional Safety

- Are differences separated from defects?
- Is severity proportionate to evidence?
- Does the report avoid destiny, diagnosis, blame, and fear-based language?

### 15.8 Action

- Does every action trace back to a prior insight?
- Are the actions realistic in the product’s relationship context?
- Does the reader know where to begin?

### 15.9 Ending

- Does the closing integrate rather than add new complexity?
- Does the reader finish with greater clarity?
- Does the report leave grounded hope and agency?
- Is the final emotional result consistent with **“AHA! It’s ME.”**?

## 17. Future Evolution

This document defines the experience architecture rather than the interface itself.

Future Relationship Products should inherit this experience instead of redesigning it.

Every new product may answer a different relationship question, but it should guide the reader through the same fundamental movement:

```text
Recognition
↓
Understanding
↓
Insight
↓
Perspective
↓
Agency
↓
Hope
```

New components, visualizations, AI capabilities, and content formats may evolve. The emotional logic should remain stable unless research or product evidence proves that the canonical journey itself must change.

The goal is consistency of experience, not uniformity of content.

## Final Principle

People rarely remember every score or every sentence.

They remember the moment something finally made sense.

A Relationship Report should never feel like software displaying conclusions.

It should feel like someone gradually helping the reader understand themselves, understand another person, and see the relationship more clearly.

If the reader naturally finishes the experience thinking,

> **“AHA! It’s ME.”**

then the experience has succeeded.
