## 0. Product Manifesto

People do not come to Ahaitsme Work because they want to be graded as employees.

They come because they want to understand:

- why some work feels natural while other work drains them,
- why they lead well in one situation but struggle in another,
- why one coworker makes them sharper while another makes every task heavier,
- why conflict with a colleague remains awkward even after the issue appears resolved,
- why they can start boldly but struggle to organize or finish,
- why they are strong with people but weak with systems—or the reverse,
- what kind of help, structure, role, team, and environment allow them to do their best work,
- and how to use themselves more wisely.

Ahaitsme Work does not evaluate job performance.

It does not predict promotion, income, business success, or professional status.

It does not tell users that they are destined for one profession, company type, or career path.

Instead, it helps users recognize how they tend to operate at work, how two people tend to collaborate, where a team may become stronger or more costly, and what structure can help each person contribute more effectively.

The user should move through the experience thinking:

> “맞아, 나는 일을 시작할 때는 빠른데 정리에서 자주 막혀.”  
> “그래서 이 사람과 일하면 결과는 좋은데 유난히 피곤했구나.”  
> “이 사람은 사과보다 다시 정확히 맞춰 일하는 게 중요했구나.”  
> “내가 못하는 사람이 아니라, 도움을 받아야 하는 지점이 분명했던 거구나.”  
> “이 역할에서는 내가 훨씬 잘 쓰일 수 있겠다.”

We are not building a career fortune report.

We are building a **work operating system for people and teams**.

The final goal is not to label the user.

The final goal is:

> **to help the user understand how to use themselves well.**

# 1. Product Definition

## 1.1 What this product is

Ahaitsme Work is a guided interpretation experience that translates supported Saju evidence, real-world Psych evidence, and pair dynamics into a practical operating manual for work.

It answers five enduring human questions:

1. **How do I naturally work?**
2. **Where do I create value, and where do I need support?**
3. **How do we work together?**
4. **What happens when work becomes difficult or awkward?**
5. **What structure, role, and environment help us work better?**

These questions are the stable foundation of the product.

They may be expressed through individual, pair, and future team experiences, but they must remain connected to one North Star:

> **Help people use themselves and each other more wisely.**

## 1.2 What this product is not

Ahaitsme Work is not:

- an employee evaluation,
- a productivity score,
- a performance review,
- a promotion predictor,
- a leadership rank,
- a hiring recommendation engine,
- an aptitude test that selects one correct profession,
- a deterministic career forecast,
- a business-success prediction,
- an HR risk classifier,
- a personality-type label collection,
- a dashboard of raw Saju or Psych values,
- or a collection of generic career advice.

The product may use axes, structured evidence, comparison tables, maps, and signals internally and visually.

Those elements must support human understanding. They must not become a hidden ranking system.

## 1.3 Product promise

The product promise is:

> **“We help you understand how you work best, how collaboration changes you, and what structure helps your strengths become useful.”**

Users should feel:

> “This does not merely describe my traits. It explains how to use them at work.”

The product must deliver four outcomes:

### Recognition

The user recognizes familiar work behaviors, energy patterns, strengths, and recurring difficulties.

### Reframing

The user understands that a difficulty may be a mismatch of role, structure, timing, or support—not proof of incompetence.

### Coordination

The user sees how two people’s styles combine, where they reinforce each other, and where translation or role design is required.

### Action

The user leaves with practical guidance about role division, communication, conflict repair, support, and environmental fit.

## 1.4 Product North Star

The Work product’s North Star is:

> **“나를 가장 잘 쓰는 방법.”**  
> **“The best way to use myself well.”**

Every major feature must help the user answer at least one of these:

- Where am I naturally effective?
- What conditions help that strength appear?
- Where am I likely to miss, delay, overdo, or become depleted?
- What support makes my work more reliable?
- Which kinds of people complement me?
- How should we divide responsibility?
- How should conflict or awkwardness be repaired?
- What should I practice to become easier to work with?
- What kinds of environments are worth exploring?

If a feature does not help the user use themselves, a coworker, or a team more wisely, it does not belong in the core Work product.

# 2. Product Scope and Boundaries

## 2.1 Work owns

Work owns the experience of functioning with other people in a professional or task-oriented context:

- work energy,
- task approach,
- starting, maintaining, and finishing,
- planning and execution,
- decision-making,
- thinking style,
- structure and organization,
- detail attention,
- practical judgment,
- self-control under pressure,
- communication and feedback,
- recognition needs,
- conflict behavior,
- repair after tension,
- leadership expression,
- coordination,
- role complementarity,
- collaboration cost,
- team contribution,
- and exploratory environment fit.

## 2.2 Work does not own

Work must not claim authority over:

- actual job performance,
- professional competence verified through work samples,
- compensation,
- promotion likelihood,
- disciplinary decisions,
- hiring or termination decisions,
- medical or psychological diagnosis,
- legal employment advice,
- objective occupational licensing suitability,
- or guaranteed business outcomes.

The product may suggest environments or roles to explore.

It must not decide a user’s career for them.

## 2.3 Individual, pair, and team boundary

The Work product contains three connected lenses:

### Me — How I Work

The user’s work operating manual.

### Us — How We Work

The dynamic created between two coworkers, collaborators, leaders, or partners.

### Team — How We Combine

A future expansion that examines role coverage, overlap, coordination cost, and missing functions across more than two people.

The individual lens must not become a generic personality report.

The pair lens must not become a compatibility grade.

The team lens must not become an automated hiring or employee-ranking tool.

## 2.4 Supported career-environment exploration

The product may explore possible fit with environments such as:

- large organizations,
- startups,
- entrepreneurship,
- public or institutional environments,
- research-heavy work,
- operations,
- field or physical work,
- client-facing work,
- international or cross-cultural work,
- independent work,
- and highly collaborative teams.

These must be framed as:

- conditions that may suit the user,
- hypotheses worth testing,
- or environments where certain tendencies may become useful.

They must not be framed as destiny or guaranteed success.

Preferred language:

> “자율성과 빠른 의사결정이 있는 환경에서 강점을 쓰기 쉬울 가능성이 있습니다.”

Avoid:

> “당신은 반드시 스타트업을 해야 성공합니다.”

# 3. Product Laws

These laws are permanent. Any feature, design, copy, resolver, model output, or visualization that violates them is out of scope.

## Law 1 — Usefulness before evaluation

The product must help the user operate better.

It must not grade their worth, competence, leadership, or professional potential.

## Law 2 — The person is not the problem

A weakness may reflect:

- role mismatch,
- insufficient support,
- poor task design,
- timing mismatch,
- unclear ownership,
- energy mismatch,
- or a missing complementary strength.

The product may name limitations clearly, but must not reduce a person to a deficiency.

## Law 3 — “Strong” does not mean “good at everything”

Every strength has:

- a best use,
- a likely overuse,
- a context where it becomes costly,
- and a support need.

Example:

> Strong initiation may create momentum, but without structure or detail support it may leave unfinished work behind.

## Law 4 — Difference is not incompetence

Two people may differ in:

- speed,
- detail,
- caution,
- communication,
- decision-making,
- recognition needs,
- or conflict recovery.

The product must explain the operational consequence of the difference without naming one person as the better worker.

## Law 5 — Pair dynamics are not individual traits

The main question is not only:

> “What is A like?” and “What is B like?”

It is:

> “What happens when A and B work together?”

The collaboration is an emergent system.

## Law 6 — Real-world Psych and Saju remain distinct

The Psych 11 axes are real-world survey or psych evidence.

They are not derived from Saju.

Saju evidence and Psych evidence may:

- support the same interpretation,
- qualify each other,
- contradict each other,
- or answer different questions.

Their source identity and limitations must remain visible internally and clear in the experience.

## Law 7 — Use only relevant Psych axes

All eleven axes do not need to appear in every section.

A product or module may use an axis only when it:

- answers a real user question,
- materially changes interpretation or advice,
- has supported direction,
- and does not merely repeat another visible insight.

The full 11-axis overview may remain available as an evidence view, but the primary narrative must be selective.

## Law 8 — Saju comparison must become human meaning

A Saju-derived comparison table must not display decorative symbols or raw terms without interpretation.

It must explain:

- A’s likely tendency,
- B’s likely tendency,
- what happens between them,
- when the difference helps,
- when translation is needed,
- and what role or support follows.

## Law 9 — Every visual must explain itself

No radar, table, map, chart, axis, score, color, or quadrant is self-explanatory.

Every major visual must state:

- what it represents,
- what each direction means,
- why the user or pair appears there,
- what evidence contributed,
- and what the user should do with the insight.

## Law 10 — Type, not rank

Colors, positions, labels, quadrants, and shapes identify work patterns.

They do not identify winners, losers, good workers, bad workers, or better teams.

Danger styling is reserved for independently supported cost, strain, or risk overlays.

## Law 11 — Recognition comes before advice

The correct order is:

1. show the familiar work pattern,
2. explain why it occurs,
3. show the person or pair interpretation,
4. then recommend a structure, response, or experiment.

Generic advice before recognition is not premium.

## Law 12 — Conflict repair is a core work skill

The product must not stop at describing how conflict starts.

It must explain how different people:

- need acknowledgment,
- need privacy,
- need time,
- need direct apology,
- need action,
- need clarification,
- need a reset ritual,
- or prefer to resume work through reliable behavior.

The repair guidance must remain evidence-aware and non-stereotyped.

## Law 13 — The product may be direct

Work users need clarity.

The product may say:

- where a person often loses detail,
- where organization breaks down,
- where leadership becomes controlling,
- where caution becomes delay,
- where speed becomes rework,
- or where emotional avoidance creates operational confusion.

Directness must preserve dignity and include a practical response.

## Law 14 — No unsupported occupational certainty

The product must not claim that Saju or Psych alone can determine:

- a correct profession,
- a correct employer,
- business success,
- promotion,
- income,
- or professional status.

Career and environment suggestions remain exploratory.

## Law 15 — The final output is an operating manual

The user should finish with a clear answer to:

> “How should I use myself, and how should others work with me?”

If the user only remembers labels or charts, the product has failed.

# 4. Core Experience Model

## 4.1 The five content territories

### Territory A — How I Work

User question:

> “나는 일을 어떻게 시작하고, 생각하고, 결정하고, 끝내는 사람인가?”

Core material:

- work energy,
- stimulation needs,
- thinking style,
- decision style,
- planning,
- execution,
- structure,
- practicality,
- detail attention,
- persistence,
- and response to pressure.

Emotional outcome:

> “내가 잘 작동하는 방식과 막히는 지점을 알겠다.”

### Territory B — Where I Create Value

User question:

> “나는 어떤 방식으로 팀에 기여하고, 어디에서 도움을 받아야 하는가?”

Core material:

- strongest contribution,
- leadership expression,
- people connection,
- system organization,
- research depth,
- field or physical engagement,
- initiation,
- maintenance,
- completion,
- quality control,
- and complementary support.

Emotional outcome:

> “내 강점의 쓰임과 약점의 보완법이 보인다.”

### Territory C — How We Work Together

User question:

> “이 사람과 나는 함께 일할 때 어떤 조합이 되는가?”

Core material:

- Collaboration Map,
- collaboration power,
- collaboration comfort,
- role complementarity,
- overlap,
- coordination cost,
- decision ownership,
- communication fit,
- planning–execution balance,
- and pair-specific Saju and Psych comparison.

Emotional outcome:

> “좋고 나쁜 궁합이 아니라, 이 조합을 어떻게 운영해야 하는지 알겠다.”

### Territory D — What Happens Under Pressure

User question:

> “문제가 생기거나 관계가 어색해졌을 때 우리는 어떻게 반응하고 풀어가는가?”

Core material:

- conflict entry,
- pressure response,
- stopping vs pushing,
- directness vs withdrawal,
- feedback sensitivity,
- apology and acknowledgment,
- recovery timing,
- residual awkwardness,
- action-based repair,
- and restart rules.

Emotional outcome:

> “갈등을 피하는 대신, 이 사람에게 맞는 회복 방식을 알겠다.”

### Territory E — How to Use This Better

User question:

> “그래서 내일 무엇을 다르게 할 수 있는가?”

Core material:

- role design,
- task ownership,
- support request,
- communication rule,
- meeting or feedback adjustment,
- decision protocol,
- conflict repair move,
- growth practice,
- and environment-fit experiments.

Emotional outcome:

> “이 분석을 실제 일에 적용할 수 있다.”

## 4.2 The experience sequence

The recommended journey is:

1. **Recognition** — “이게 내 일하는 방식이다.”
2. **Value** — “이 부분에서 내가 강하다.”
3. **Limit** — “이 지점에서는 도움이나 구조가 필요하다.”
4. **Combination** — “이 사람과 함께하면 이런 조합이 된다.”
5. **Pressure** — “문제가 생기면 이런 패턴이 나타난다.”
6. **Repair** — “이 방식으로 다시 맞출 수 있다.”
7. **Design** — “역할과 환경을 이렇게 설계하면 된다.”
8. **Experiment** — “이번 주에 이것을 시험해본다.”

Strength must appear before weakness.

Weakness must be explained before advice.

Pair type must be explained before warning overlays.

Career suggestions must appear after the operating pattern is established.

# 5. Product Layers

## 5.1 Layer 1 — Career Operating Manual

This is the individual foundation.

It answers:

- How do I take in information?
- How do I make decisions?
- What gives me energy?
- What drains me?
- What kind of ownership suits me?
- Where do I need structure?
- What kind of feedback helps?
- How do I behave under pressure?
- What should I delegate or check?
- Which environments are worth testing?

This layer may be usable alone.

## 5.2 Layer 2 — Collaboration Operating Manual

This is the two-person experience.

It answers:

- What kind of collaboration is this?
- Where do we create stronger results together?
- Where does communication become expensive?
- What roles should be separated?
- What decisions need clear ownership?
- What does each person need during conflict?
- How can we restart after awkwardness?
- What should we not expect the other person to do naturally?

## 5.3 Layer 3 — Team Design

This is a future expansion.

It may answer:

- Which functions are naturally covered?
- Which functions are duplicated?
- Which role is missing?
- Where is decision ownership unclear?
- Which person becomes overloaded?
- Which pair creates coordination friction?
- What support structure is needed?

It must not:

- rank employees,
- automate hiring,
- select who should be fired,
- or present deterministic team-success scores.

# 6. Hero Experience

## 6.1 Individual Hero

The individual Hero answers:

> “What kind of worker am I when I am functioning well?”

Required content:

1. name,
2. concise work signature,
3. strongest contribution,
4. one condition that activates it,
5. one caution or support need,
6. invitation into the operating manual.

Example form:

> **새로운 방향을 만들고 사람을 움직이는 시작형 기획자**  
> 방향이 불분명한 초기에 강하지만, 세부 정리와 반복 관리에서는 구조적 지원이 있을 때 성과가 안정됩니다.

This is not a job title.

It is an operating signature.

## 6.2 Pair Hero

The pair Hero answers:

> “What kind of collaboration is this?”

It should contain:

1. two names,
2. collaboration pattern,
3. one-line relational truth,
4. one strength,
5. one operational tension,
6. invitation to the Collaboration Map.

Examples:

- 편하고 강한 조합
- 편하지만 빈틈이 있는 조합
- 불편하지만 강력한 조합
- 역할 설계가 필요한 조합

These are types, not grades.

## 6.3 Warning overlays

“지속 비용이 큰 조합” is not a fifth equal pair type.

It is an overlay state that may appear on any base type when supported.

Possible overlays:

- high energy cost,
- unclear ownership,
- decision bottleneck,
- repeated repair failure,
- quality-risk concentration,
- recognition deficit,
- or unresolved tension.

Example:

> **불편하지만 강력한 조합**  
> ⚠ 현재 지속 비용이 높은 상태

The warning must explain:

- what creates the cost,
- whether it is structural or situational,
- and what may reduce it.

# 7. Core Experience Architecture

## Module 1 — Work Signature

**Human question:** “나는 어떤 방식으로 일할 때 가장 강한가?”

Purpose:

- immediate recognition,
- establish the operating signature,
- identify one best-use condition,
- and create curiosity.

Primary assets:

- work signature,
- one-line contribution,
- strength condition,
- caution condition.

Success test:

The user can repeat their work signature without treating it as a fixed identity.

## Module 2 — Work Shape

**Human question:** “내 일하는 방식의 전체 모양은 어떤가?”

Purpose:

- show the broad Psych profile,
- reveal meaningful balance and imbalance,
- orient the user before deeper narrative.

Primary assets:

- 11-axis radar or profile shape,
- selective axis highlights,
- plain-language axis explanations,
- confidence and completeness notes.

Rules:

- The 11 axes are Psych evidence, not Saju.
- The radar is evidence overview, not a score.
- The narrative must explain the most relevant axes.
- Do not force all eleven axes into the main story.

Success test:

The user understands why two or three axes matter to their actual work.

## Module 3 — Strengths in Use

**Human question:** “내 강점은 어떤 상황에서 실제 가치가 되는가?”

Purpose:

- translate tendencies into contribution,
- distinguish capability from context,
- show best use and likely overuse.

Potential contribution dimensions:

- direction setting,
- planning,
- rapid execution,
- sustained operation,
- completion,
- quality control,
- people connection,
- negotiation,
- research,
- synthesis,
- field response,
- coordination,
- and stabilization.

Preferred structure:

| Strength | Best use | Overuse risk | Support that helps |
|---|---|---|---|

Success test:

The user knows where to volunteer, lead, contribute, or ask for support.

## Module 4 — Friction and Support Needs

**Human question:** “어디에서 자주 막히고, 어떤 도움이 있으면 달라지는가?”

Purpose:

- name limitations without shame,
- distinguish weakness from unsupported environment,
- define specific support.

Examples:

- strong leadership but weak detail control,
- strong ideas but poor organization,
- deep research but slow decision,
- fast action but high rework,
- high empathy but difficult boundary-setting,
- strong self-control but delayed escalation notice,
- high recognition need but reluctance to ask.

Primary assets:

- support map,
- “do myself / share / delegate / check” table,
- failure-pattern scene,
- one practical safeguard.

Success test:

The user can request a specific kind of support rather than saying “I am bad at this.”

## Module 5 — Work Mode Comparison

**Human question:** “나는 어떤 종류의 일에 자연스럽게 에너지를 쓰는가?”

Purpose:

- compare work modes without selecting one profession,
- identify where work feels natural, effortful, or developmental.

Possible comparisons:

- big-picture ↔ detail,
- planning ↔ execution,
- start ↔ maintain ↔ finish,
- people ↔ systems,
- field ↔ desk,
- physical ↔ conceptual,
- research depth ↔ rapid iteration,
- independent ↔ coordinated,
- stable routine ↔ changing environment,
- local focus ↔ cross-cultural or international context.

Evidence may come from:

- supported Saju tendencies,
- selected Psych axes,
- or cross-source synthesis.

Rules:

- source identity must remain traceable,
- categories are not mutually exclusive,
- no profession is declared as destiny.

Success test:

The user identifies two environments or task modes worth testing.

## Module 6 — Collaboration Map

**Human question:** “우리는 함께 일할 때 어떤 조합인가?”

Purpose:

- give immediate pair orientation,
- show the pair’s current position,
- distinguish collaboration type from cost.

Primary visual:

### Two-axis Collaboration Map

Vertical axis:

> **결과를 만드는 힘 / Collaboration Power**

Low:

> 함께 일해도 강점이 결합되지 않거나 결과가 분산되기 쉽다.

High:

> 함께 있을 때 각자의 강점이 결합되어 더 큰 결과를 만들 가능성이 높다.

Horizontal axis:

> **협업의 편안함 / Collaboration Comfort**

Low:

> 설명, 조정, 감정 관리, 의사결정에 지속적인 에너지가 든다.

High:

> 소통과 역할 조율이 비교적 자연스럽고 함께 일하는 비용이 낮다.

Quadrants:

| Position | Base type |
|---|---|
| High power / High comfort | 편하고 강한 조합 |
| High power / Low comfort | 불편하지만 강력한 조합 |
| Low power / High comfort | 편하지만 빈틈이 있는 조합 |
| Low power / Low comfort | 역할 설계가 필요한 조합 |

The map must show:

- the full field,
- the pair’s point,
- axis explanations,
- why the point is there,
- relevant evidence,
- and improvement direction where supported.

Numbers, if used, are coordinates—not performance scores.

Success test:

The user can explain how the pair works without asking whether the result is “good” or “bad.”

## Module 7 — Two-Person Comparison

**Human question:** “우리는 어떤 점에서 비슷하고, 다르고, 보완되는가?”

Purpose:

- provide fast A/B understanding,
- combine selective Psych comparison and separate Saju comparison,
- translate difference into operational consequence.

### Psych comparison

Use only relevant axes such as:

- structure,
- practicality,
- self-control,
- empathy,
- conflict style,
- resilience,
- recognition,
- energy style,
- thinking style,
- decision style,
- stimulation.

### Saju comparison

The Saju-derived table may examine supported tendencies such as:

- scope and perspective,
- initiation,
- maintenance,
- completion,
- organization,
- detail,
- research depth,
- action speed,
- people connection,
- system building,
- pressure response,
- persistence,
- leadership expression,
- and environmental orientation.

Preferred table:

| Work question | Person A | Person B | What happens together |
|---|---|---|---|

Meaning categories:

- shared ground,
- complementary difference,
- translation required,
- role-design need,
- friction watchpoint.

Rules:

- Psych and Saju must remain separate evidence families.
- Do not present Saju as survey psychology.
- Do not display raw traditional terminology without human interpretation.
- Do not compare to crown a stronger person.

Success test:

The pair understands at least one role division and one translation need.

## Module 8 — Role Design

**Human question:** “누가 무엇을 맡을 때 이 조합이 가장 잘 작동하는가?”

Purpose:

- turn comparison into operating structure,
- reduce duplicated ownership,
- protect weak points.

Potential role functions:

- direction,
- planning,
- decision,
- execution,
- coordination,
- documentation,
- quality check,
- relationship management,
- research,
- risk review,
- and completion.

Preferred output:

### Natural ownership

What each person may naturally own.

### Shared ownership

What requires joint review.

### Support ownership

Where one person should check, structure, or complete the other’s work.

### Dangerous ambiguity

Where unclear ownership may produce delay, resentment, or missed work.

Success test:

The pair can change one responsibility rule.

## Module 9 — Decision and Communication Flow

**Human question:** “우리는 어떻게 생각하고 결정하고 전달할 때 덜 엇갈리는가?”

Purpose:

- explain information flow,
- prevent speed and detail mismatch,
- define communication protocols.

Core material:

- thinking style,
- decision style,
- structure,
- practicality,
- empathy,
- recognition,
- expression pattern,
- and leadership direction.

Possible outputs:

- when to send written context,
- when to talk live,
- who drafts first,
- who reviews,
- how much detail is needed,
- when a decision is final,
- who communicates outward,
- and how disagreement is recorded.

Success test:

The user has one concrete meeting, decision, or handoff rule.

## Module 10 — Pressure Pattern

**Human question:** “문제가 생기면 각자는 어떻게 반응하는가?”

Purpose:

- reveal non-obvious pressure behavior,
- distinguish useful persistence from blind pushing,
- distinguish useful pause from avoidance.

Possible patterns:

- stop and assess,
- push forward,
- gather more information,
- seek people,
- take control,
- withdraw,
- solve alone,
- externalize urgency,
- over-document,
- or delay commitment.

Preferred structure:

1. trigger,
2. Person A’s response,
3. Person B’s interpretation,
4. Person B’s response,
5. operational consequence,
6. interruption point.

Success test:

The pair can recognize the pattern before it becomes a conflict.

## Module 11 — Conflict and Awkwardness Repair

**Human question:** “업무상 관계가 어색해졌을 때 우리는 어떻게 다시 일할 수 있는가?”

Purpose:

- treat repair as an operating skill,
- distinguish apology, acknowledgment, time, action, and restart needs,
- prevent unresolved awkwardness from becoming collaboration cost.

Potential repair preferences:

- direct 1:1 conversation,
- explicit apology,
- acknowledgment of impact,
- explanation and clarification,
- visible corrective action,
- practical gesture,
- food or a small relational gesture,
- processing time,
- written follow-up,
- or resuming reliable work with a clear reset.

Important:

A gesture such as food may support repair, but must not substitute for accountability where accountability is required.

Required outputs:

- first move,
- timing,
- what not to force,
- preferred acknowledgment,
- evidence of repair,
- restart sentence,
- and completion signal.

Success test:

The user could use the guide after a real workplace conflict.

## Module 12 — Feedback and Recognition Manual

**Human question:** “어떤 피드백과 인정 방식이 이 사람을 움직이게 하는가?”

Purpose:

- reduce avoidable defensiveness,
- distinguish public and private feedback,
- translate recognition needs into professional behavior.

Possible topics:

- public vs private feedback,
- direct vs contextual delivery,
- immediate vs scheduled review,
- evidence-first vs empathy-first,
- autonomy after feedback,
- explicit recognition,
- quiet trust,
- and correction follow-through.

Rules:

- recognition need is not vanity,
- low recognition need is not emotional superiority,
- feedback guidance must remain professional and realistic.

Success test:

The user can deliver one piece of feedback more effectively.

## Module 13 — Leadership and Followership

**Human question:** “나는 어떤 방식으로 이끌고, 어떤 리더 아래에서 잘 일하는가?”

Purpose:

- describe leadership expression rather than leadership rank,
- show where authority helps or hurts,
- examine receiving direction as well as giving it.

Possible leadership expressions:

- directional,
- strategic,
- operational,
- coaching,
- relational,
- stabilizing,
- crisis-led,
- or expertise-led.

Required balance:

- best use,
- overuse risk,
- team need,
- decision boundary,
- and preferred leader relationship.

Success test:

The user understands both how to lead and how to be led.

## Module 14 — Environment Fit Explorer

**Human question:** “어떤 환경에서 내 방식이 더 잘 쓰일 가능성이 있는가?”

Purpose:

- offer hypotheses,
- connect tendencies to conditions,
- encourage real-world testing.

Possible environments:

- large organization,
- startup,
- entrepreneurship,
- research,
- operations,
- public institution,
- field work,
- client service,
- international or cross-cultural work,
- independent work,
- high-change work,
- and stable repeatable systems.

For every suggestion include:

- why it may fit,
- what may become difficult,
- what evidence supports the hypothesis,
- and what to test before making a decision.

Success test:

The user leaves with experiments, not a deterministic career verdict.

## Module 15 — Your Work Manual

**Human question:** “나를 가장 잘 쓰는 방법은 무엇인가?”

Purpose:

- synthesize the full report,
- create re-read value,
- end with clarity and agency.

Recommended structure:

### Use me here

Where the user is most useful.

### Give me this

Conditions, information, authority, and support required.

### Watch me here

Likely overuse, blind spots, and failure patterns.

### Pair me with

Complementary coworker styles or functions.

### Do not assume

Common misreadings about the user.

### When conflict happens

Short repair instructions.

### This week

One small experiment.

Success test:

The user can save or share the manual and use it in real work.

# 9. Evidence Principles

## 9.1 Evidence families

The Work product may use:

### Psych person evidence

The real-world six primary and eleven secondary axes.

### Psych pair evidence

Similarity, gap, complementarity, directional mismatch, and context-specific amplification.

### Saju person evidence

Supported work-relevant tendencies translated into behavior.

### Saju pair evidence

Supported pair interactions and role dynamics.

### Cross-source evidence

Independent Psych and Saju evidence supporting or refining the same work interpretation.

### Explicit work context

User-provided role, project, organization, or relationship context.

Explicit context personalizes the interpretation but must not be confused with inferred truth.

## 9.2 The Psych 11 axes

Canonical axes:

- stimulation,
- self_control,
- practicality,
- structure,
- empathy,
- conflict_style,
- resilience,
- recognition,
- energy_style,
- thinking_style,
- decision_style.

These are real-world Psych evidence.

They must not be:

- renamed as Saju-derived traits,
- silently reconstructed from Saju,
- or treated as a complete measure of professional ability.

## 9.3 Selective use by question

Examples:

| Human question | Likely relevant axes |
|---|---|
| How do I organize work? | structure, practicality, self_control |
| How do I make decisions? | thinking_style, decision_style, practicality |
| How do I respond under pressure? | resilience, conflict_style, self_control |
| How do I work with people? | empathy, energy_style, recognition |
| Why does feedback hurt or fail? | recognition, empathy, conflict_style |
| Why is this collaboration tiring? | energy_style, conflict_style, decision_style, structure |
| Why do we miss deadlines? | structure, self_control, practicality, decision_style |
| Why are meetings unproductive? | thinking_style, energy_style, decision_style, empathy |

This mapping is guidance, not a fixed universal rule.

## 9.4 Evidence confidence

Every major interpretation should reflect evidence quality:

### Corroborated

Independent evidence supports the same meaning.

### Refined

The direction is supported but conditions or contradictions matter.

### Tentative

The evidence supports an exploratory hypothesis.

### Unsupported

Omit.

Career-environment suggestions should usually remain refined or tentative unless supported by strong multi-source evidence and explicit real-world context.

## 9.5 Capability claims

The product must distinguish:

### Tendency

How a person may approach work.

### Preference

What environment or mode may feel more natural.

### Contribution

How the tendency may create value.

### Capability

What the person can demonstrably do.

Saju and Psych may support tendency, preference, and possible contribution.

They do not independently prove professional capability.

# 10. Storytelling Rules

## 10.1 Scene before label

Bad:

> “A has low structure and high stimulation.”

Better:

> “새로운 일이 시작될 때는 빠르게 움직이지만, 파일 정리와 반복 점검이 이어지면 집중이 쉽게 분산될 수 있습니다.”

Then show relevant Psych evidence.

## 10.2 Meaning before machinery

The user should first understand:

- the work scene,
- the operational consequence,
- and the support needed.

Then they may inspect:

- axes,
- Saju evidence,
- confidence,
- or comparison detail.

## 10.3 One insight, one job

Every major module must answer one human question.

Do not combine:

- career environment,
- conflict repair,
- leadership,
- and role division

into one long card merely because the evidence overlaps.

## 10.4 Strength–cost pairing

Every major strength should include:

- useful expression,
- overuse,
- and support.

Every major difficulty should include:

- context,
- reframe,
- and action.

## 10.5 No excerpt rewriting

Narrative synthesis should use more than one meaningful evidence source when possible.

It must not merely rewrite:

- one axis value,
- one Saju line,
- or one resolver output.

## 10.6 Pair-specific language

Ask:

> Could this sentence apply unchanged to most coworkers?

If yes, it is too generic.

Pair narrative must explain:

- direction,
- interaction,
- context,
- and consequence.

# 11. Copy Principles

## 11.1 Tone

The Work voice should feel like:

- a perceptive operator,
- a practical coach,
- and a fair colleague

combined into one narrator.

It should be:

- warm,
- intelligent,
- concise,
- direct,
- non-clinical,
- and useful.

It may be firmer than Romantic.

It must never become harsh, corporate, mystical, or patronizing.

## 11.2 Address law

Use names whenever possible.

Avoid “Person A / Person B” in final narrative.

Role labels may be used when clearer:

- 방향을 잡는 쪽,
- 실행을 밀어붙이는 쪽,
- 검토하는 쪽,
- 정리하는 쪽,
- 먼저 사과가 필요한 쪽,
- 시간이 필요한 쪽.

## 11.3 Tendency, not fixed identity

Preferred:

- “~하는 편입니다”
- “이런 상황에서 ~가 나타나기 쉽습니다”
- “구조가 없을 때 ~할 가능성이 있습니다”
- “두 사람이 함께 일하면 ~로 이어질 수 있습니다”

Avoid:

- “원래 정리를 못합니다”
- “리더가 될 운명입니다”
- “사업해야 성공합니다”
- “대기업 체질입니다”
- “절대 같이 일하면 안 됩니다”

## 11.4 No corporate euphemism

Do not hide useful truth behind vague language.

Bad:

> “세부적인 부분에서는 성장 가능성이 있습니다.”

Better:

> “큰 방향을 빠르게 잡는 대신, 세부 확인을 건너뛰어 재작업이 생길 수 있습니다. 최종 제출 전 다른 사람의 체크가 있으면 성과가 안정됩니다.”

## 11.5 Humor

Light humor may expose familiar work patterns.

Examples:

- a meeting where one person wants the answer and the other wants twelve more slides,
- a handoff where both assumed the other person owned the final check,
- a conflict where “괜찮습니다” clearly does not mean the same thing to both people.

Humor must not:

- mock competence,
- shame vulnerability,
- or turn a coworker into a caricature.

## 11.6 Locale is an interpretation layer, not an evidence layer

Product surfaces:

- `ahaitsme.com` → default locale `en-US` (primarily North American users)
- `ahaitsme.com/kr` → default locale `ko-KR` (primarily Korean users)

**Same evidence → same canonical judgment → locale-aware explanation, example, script, and action.**

Locale may change only:

- wording,
- workplace examples,
- communication framing,
- recommended scripts,
- action guidance,
- and explanatory emphasis.

Locale must **not** change:

- Saju calculations,
- psychometric axis values,
- pair signals,
- score formulas,
- canonical classifications,
- confidence or severity,
- or section inclusion logic (unless an existing locale contract explicitly requires otherwise).

Locale must never be used as a proxy for nationality, ethnicity, company culture, job level, gender, or management status.

Cultural framing defaults (interpretation only):

- **en-US:** peer and cross-functional collaboration; direct but respectful feedback; productive disagreement; ownership and clear expectations; meeting and async handoffs; manager/direct-report language only when evidence or the section already supports it.
- **ko-KR:** honorific and face-sensitive communication; awareness of rank/tenure when relevant to the script; softer disagreement entry; private feedback over public correction; pre-/post-meeting alignment — without treating hierarchy as universally desirable or recommending silence/obedience.

Omitted locale in Work generators follows the documented legacy fallback (`ko-KR`), not a silent flip to English.
