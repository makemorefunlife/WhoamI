## 0. Product Manifesto

People do not come to Ahaitsme Friend because they want to know whether a friend is “good” or “bad.”

They come because they want to understand:

- why this friend feels different from other friends,
- why they instinctively reach for this person in certain moments,
- what this friend brings into their life,
- what they themselves bring into the friend’s life,
- why some differences feel supportive while others become tiring,
- when this friendship is most dependable, useful, joyful, or transformative,
- and how to protect the best parts of the friendship without ignoring its costs.

Ahaitsme Friend does not grade friendships.

It does not rank friends.

It does not tell users who their “best friend” should be.

It does not reduce a person to a personality label or a single symbolic role.

Instead, it helps users recognize the lived function of a friendship.

The user should move through the experience thinking:

> “아, 그래서 힘들 때 이 친구가 생각났구나.”  
> “이 친구는 나한테 이런 걸 가져다주는 사람이었네.”  
> “나도 이 친구한테 이런 역할을 하고 있었구나.”  
> “이 차이가 꼭 나쁜 건 아니었네.”  
> “이럴 때는 이 친구의 강점이 진짜 빛나는구나.”  
> “앞으로는 이 친구에게 이렇게 말하거나 기대하면 되겠다.”

We are not building a friendship score.

We are building a **friendship experience**.

The user should not feel like they are reading two personality profiles placed side by side. They should feel like they are seeing the friendship itself: what each person contributes, what becomes possible between them, when the bond is most useful, where it becomes costly, and how both people can bring out more of its value.

When the experience ends, the user should remember not a score, but a person-shaped truth:

> “이 친구는 내가 너무 힘들고 울고 싶을 때, 말보다 곁을 지켜주는 든든한 내 편이 될 수 있는 사람이다.”

# 1. Product Definition

## 1.1 What this product is

Ahaitsme Friend is a guided relationship experience that translates Saju relationship evidence, 11-axis psychological evidence, and pair-specific interaction patterns into a clear human narrative about friendship.

It answers six enduring human questions:

1. **What does this friend bring into my life?**
2. **What do I bring into this friend’s life?**
3. **When does this friendship work at its best?**
4. **What becomes difficult between us, and why?**
5. **How do we bring out the best in each other?**
6. **Who do we become when we are together?**

These questions are the stable content foundation of the Friend product.

They are not displayed as six rigid chapters. They are woven into a story that moves from recognition to mutual value, situational usefulness, friction, and practical relationship care.

---

## 1.2 What this product is not

Ahaitsme Friend is not:

- a friendship compatibility score,
- a ranking of who is the better friend,
- a “best friend / bad friend” classifier,
- a list of personality traits,
- a static label such as “the practical friend” or “the emotional friend,”
- a prophecy about how long the friendship will last,
- a prediction of when the friendship will end,
- a horoscope-style metaphor generator,
- a generic friendship advice article,
- a social popularity score,
- a diagnostic or clinical assessment,
- or a collection of disconnected cards.

The product may use scores, axes, signals, and structured evidence internally. Those elements must never become the user’s main experience.

---

## 1.3 Product promise

The product promise is:

> **“We help you see what this friendship gives both of you, when it matters most, and how to keep its best parts alive.”**

Users should feel:

> “This does not just describe my friend and me. It explains what this friendship does in our lives.”

The product must deliver these outcomes:

### Recognition
The user recognizes familiar scenes, roles, strengths, and recurring moments.

### Mutual value
The user sees what each person contributes without flattening either person into one role.

### Situational clarity
The user understands when the friendship’s strengths are most useful and when the same strengths may become frustrating.

### Reframing
The user sees difference as context-dependent rather than automatically good or bad.

### Action
The user leaves with a clearer way to ask, respond, support, protect, or enjoy the friendship.

### Perspective
The user understands that a friend may be especially valuable in certain situations without claiming that the friendship belongs only to a certain life stage or has a fixed lifespan.

The product must never promise certainty about friendship permanence, betrayal, abandonment, or future closeness.

---

## 1.4 Who this product is for

The Friend product is for people who want to understand a meaningful friendship beyond surface personality matching.

| Audience | What they typically need |
|---|---|
| **Close friends** | Why the bond feels safe, energizing, grounding, or unusually easy |
| **Long-term friends** | How roles developed, what keeps the friendship strong, and where assumptions have hardened |
| **Friends under strain** | Why care is being misread and how to interrupt repeated friction |
| **New but meaningful friends** | What each person may bring and where the bond could naturally deepen |
| **Reflective solo viewers** | A clearer understanding of one friend’s place in their life |
| **Friends in transition** | How the relationship may function under distance, stress, change, or new responsibilities |

The product is not designed to determine whether a friendship should end, diagnose manipulation, or replace professional support in situations involving abuse, coercion, or safety.

# 2. Product Boundaries

## 2.1 Friend owns

Friend owns the lived experience of voluntary non-romantic companionship:

- mutual support,
- emotional companionship,
- practical help,
- shared joy,
- stimulation and novelty,
- perspective and advice,
- steadiness under pressure,
- encouragement,
- recognition,
- trust,
- loyalty,
- social energy,
- closeness and space,
- friendship-specific misunderstanding,
- growth through difference,
- and the version of each person that emerges within the friendship.

---

## 2.2 Romantic owns

Romantic owns:

- attraction,
- romantic affection,
- erotic or romantic chemistry,
- couple-specific intimacy,
- romantic reassurance,
- romantic exclusivity,
- couple conflict and repair,
- and emotional partnership as a romantic bond.

Friend must not imply romantic intention or attraction unless the product explicitly receives supported relationship-context input permitting it.

---

## 2.3 Work owns

Work owns:

- execution,
- professional decision-making,
- accountability,
- feedback,
- hierarchy,
- workplace trust,
- productivity,
- role division,
- and team performance.

Friend may describe that two friends collaborate well, but must not become a professional performance product.

---

## 2.4 Family owns

Family owns:

- inherited family roles,
- caregiving obligations,
- authority,
- dependence and independence,
- family recognition,
- home climate,
- intergenerational patterns,
- and long-term family duties.

Friend must not import obligation-heavy family logic into a voluntary friendship.

---

## 2.5 Hard boundary rule

When a topic asks:

> “What does this person bring into my voluntary personal life as a friend?”

it belongs to Friend.

When a topic asks:

> “How do these two people function as romantic partners, coworkers, or family members?”

it belongs to the corresponding product.

---

## 2.6 What this product must not claim

The Friend product must not claim to determine:

- whether a friendship will last forever,
- whether one friend will betray the other,
- whether someone is a “true friend” or “fake friend,”
- whether one person cares more,
- whether distance means the friendship is ending,
- whether one person is psychologically disordered,
- whether the user should cut someone off,
- or whether a specific future event will happen.

Where an insight touches serious harm, coercion, exploitation, or safety, the product must stay within interpretive limits and avoid treating ordinary compatibility logic as a safety assessment.

---

## 2.7 Unsupported product areas

Do not create authoritative scored modules merely because users may be curious.

These require stronger evidence before becoming formal report modules:

- friendship lifespan,
- “friendship season” as a specific age or decade,
- future separation timing,
- loyalty or betrayal risk,
- popularity or social status,
- number of friends a person should have,
- who will contact whom first in a future event,
- fixed “best friend” ranking,
- financial trustworthiness,
- or whether the friendship should continue.

A relationship may shine in certain **situations**. That must never be inflated into a prediction about a specific **life stage**.

# 3. Product Laws

These laws are permanent. Any future design, copy, model output, component, or feature that violates them is out of scope.

## Law 1 — The friendship is the protagonist

Neither individual is the full story.

The product must show:

- what one person brings,
- what the other person brings,
- what happens only when they meet,
- and how the relationship changes under different situations.

---

## Law 2 — No single signal owns the story

Constitutional multi-signal preference: `04_Assessment_Philosophy.md` §4.3.

No friendship conclusion may come solely from:

- one day-stem interaction,
- one element relationship,
- one Saju hit,
- one psychological axis,
- one score difference,
- or one generated sentence.

A major claim should be shaped by multiple approved sources whenever possible.

Saju provides relational direction and symbolic structure.

The 11 axes refine how that direction may appear in behavior.

Relationship context determines which meaning is relevant.

Confidence governs how strongly the result may be written.

---

## Law 3 — Human meaning comes before analysis machinery

Do not begin with charts, raw axes, technical categories, or elemental explanations.

Begin with a recognizable friendship truth, role, scene, or situation.

Bad:

> “This pair has high resilience and structure.”

Better:

> “When everything feels messy and overwhelming, this friend may be the person who helps you slow down, sort the problem, and stay beside you until things feel manageable again.”

Then show supporting evidence.

---

## Law 4 — A strength is incomplete without its moment

Do not describe a strength without explaining **when it matters**.

A friendship strength must be attached to a lived situation.

Examples:

- empathy → when the user needs to feel understood before receiving advice,
- practicality → when a real-world decision must be made,
- structure → when life feels disorganized,
- resilience → after failure, disappointment, or crisis,
- recognition → when effort feels unseen,
- stimulation → when life feels stagnant,
- calm energy → when the user needs quiet, depth, or recovery.

> **Do not describe a strength without its moment.**

---

## Law 5 — Situational strength is not a life-stage prediction

The product may say:

> “This friendship may be especially helpful during periods of pressure or uncertainty.”

It must not say:

> “This is a friendship for your twenties.”  
> “You will naturally drift apart in your thirties.”

unless a future product explicitly owns time-based evidence and has an approved temporal contract.

> **Do not turn a situational strength into a life-stage prediction.**

---

## Law 6 — Gift and transformation must not duplicate each other

The product must distinguish:

### What I bring
The support, perspective, energy, or stability I contribute.

### What may change in the other person
How that contribution may help the friend act, feel, or express themselves differently.

These belong in one causal flow:

```text
Your Gift
  → What the friend receives
  → What may become easier or more visible in them
```

Do not create a separate large section that repeats the same meaning under “Who they become with you.”

---

## Law 7 — Difference is context-dependent

The same trait may be a gift in one environment and a cost in another.

Examples:

- caution can create safety under pressure but feel slow during rapid change,
- blunt practicality can help with hard decisions but feel cold during emotional pain,
- high stimulation can create adventure but become exhausting during recovery,
- deep sensitivity can create intimacy but increase overload in conflict.

The product must show both the useful and costly expressions without treating either as the person’s permanent identity.

---

## Law 8 — Mutuality does not require false symmetry

Both people must retain dignity, but every section does not need equal praise.

One person may contribute more steadiness in one domain.

The other may contribute more stimulation or emotional recognition.

Fairness means accurate interpretation—not forced equality.

---

## Law 9 — Recognition comes before advice

The correct order is:

1. show the familiar moment,
2. explain what each person may be contributing,
3. show why the pattern helps or hurts,
4. then offer a practical response.

Advice without recognition feels generic.

---

## Law 10 — Every difficult insight needs a usable path

The product may name:

- emotional mismatch,
- uneven initiation,
- social-energy strain,
- practical-versus-emotional friction,
- overdependence on one person’s role,
- or disappointment caused by unspoken expectations.

It must not leave the user there.

Every difficult section needs at least one of:

- a reframe,
- a request sentence,
- a boundary,
- a timing adjustment,
- an expectation reset,
- or a way to invite the friend’s strength more effectively.

---

## Law 11 — No role becomes a prison

Friendship roles such as:

- safe harbor,
- reality anchor,
- growth catalyst,
- joy companion,
- perspective giver,
- steady witness,
- connector,
- or quiet protector

may be used only as flexible summaries.

A friend may hold a primary role, a secondary role, and context-dependent minor roles.

Do not reduce a whole person to one label.

Do not imply that a friend must always perform the role assigned to them.

---

## Law 12 — No unsupported certainty

Every strong claim must be grounded in evidence.

Language must reflect evidence quality:

- **Corroborated:** direct, confident tendency language,
- **Refined:** specific but conditional language,
- **Tentative:** possibility language,
- **Insufficient:** omit or explicitly acknowledge uncertainty.

The product must never invent a beautiful friendship story merely because it sounds emotionally satisfying.

---

## Law 13 — Saju must be translated into lived human experience

Saju concepts are internal interpretive evidence.

User-facing copy should explain:

- how support feels,
- how tension appears,
- what role one person may take,
- what situation activates the relationship,
- and what behavior the user is likely to recognize.

Do not substitute decorative elemental metaphors for interpretation.

---

## Law 14 — The product must describe capability, not obligation

A friend **can** become a dependable source of support.

A friend is not obligated to always be available, regulate the user’s emotions, solve every problem, or perform an assigned role.

Copy must preserve autonomy and healthy limits.

# 4. The Core Experience Model

## 4.1 The six content territories

The product is built from six stable territories.

### Territory A — What this friend brings me

User question:

> “What does this person become in my life?”

Core material:

- primary friendship role,
- secondary friendship role,
- emotional support,
- practical support,
- stimulation,
- recognition,
- steadiness,
- perspective,
- and pair-specific relational gifts.

Emotional outcome:

> “I understand why this friend matters to me.”

---

### Territory B — What I bring this friend

User question:

> “What do I make easier, safer, brighter, or more possible for this person?”

Core material:

- support I provide,
- energy I introduce,
- perspective I contribute,
- recognition I offer,
- stability or courage I lend,
- and the change that may become more available in the friend because of this contribution.

Emotional outcome:

> “I can see that I also have a meaningful place in this friend’s life.”

---

### Territory C — Where this friendship shines

User question:

> “When is this friendship at its best?”

Core material:

- emotional crisis,
- difficult decisions,
- new beginnings,
- long-term projects,
- social expansion,
- quiet companionship,
- recognition and encouragement,
- recovery after disappointment,
- play and adventure,
- and other supported situation categories.

Emotional outcome:

> “I know when this friendship is especially valuable.”

---

### Territory D — Where the same strengths become difficult

User question:

> “Why can something I value about this friend also frustrate me?”

Core material:

- gift-to-cost conversion,
- expectation mismatch,
- energy mismatch,
- practical-versus-emotional timing,
- initiation imbalance,
- closeness-versus-space,
- and role overuse.

Emotional outcome:

> “The problem is not that the strength is fake. It is being used in the wrong moment or expected in the wrong form.”

---

### Territory E — How to bring out the best in each other

User question:

> “How can we make the friendship work more naturally?”

Core material:

- request language,
- timing,
- support preferences,
- recognition style,
- limits,
- repair,
- invitations,
- and ways to avoid forcing one friend into a permanent service role.

Emotional outcome:

> “I know how to ask for what I need and how to support this friend more effectively.”

---

### Territory F — Who we become together

User question:

> “What version of us exists only inside this friendship?”

Core material:

- shared energy,
- mutual activation,
- joint behavior,
- relationship-only playfulness,
- courage,
- calm,
- depth,
- spontaneity,
- discipline,
- or perspective.

This territory must synthesize the pair dynamic.

It must not repeat Territories A and B word-for-word.

Emotional outcome:

> “This friendship creates something that neither person produces in exactly the same way alone.”

---

## 4.2 The narrative order

The recommended emotional sequence is:

1. **Recognition** — “This sounds like our friendship.”
2. **Role** — “This is what this friend brings into my life.”
3. **Mutuality** — “This is what I bring in return.”
4. **Situation** — “This is when our strengths matter most.”
5. **Cost** — “This is when the same strengths become tiring or mismatched.”
6. **Shared identity** — “This is who we become together.”
7. **Care** — “This is how we protect the friendship.”
8. **Possibility** — “This is what the friendship can become when used well.”

Recognition must come before advice.

Value must appear before friction.

Situational usefulness must appear before any broad interpretation about the friendship’s future.

# 4A. User Questions Canon & Priority Jobs

## 4A.1 Questions users actually bring

### Friendship meaning
- Why is this friend important to me?
- What does this person bring that other friends do not?
- Why do I reach for this friend in certain moments?
- What role does this friend play in my life?
- What role do I play in theirs?

### Support
- Is this the friend I can lean on when things are hard?
- Will they listen, solve, distract, encourage, or stay quietly beside me?
- What kind of support do they naturally give?
- What kind of support do I naturally give them?
- Why do we sometimes offer the wrong kind of help?

### Difference
- Why does one of us want to talk while the other wants space?
- Why is one friend spontaneous while the other needs a plan?
- Why does one friend bring feelings and the other bring solutions?
- Which differences help us?
- Which differences become tiring?

### Shared experience
- Why am I more playful, brave, calm, talkative, or focused with this friend?
- What do we bring out in each other?
- What activities or situations fit us naturally?
- Why does the friendship feel different in private versus in a group?

### Friction and care
- Why do I sometimes feel unseen or taken for granted?
- Why does one person always initiate?
- Why can practical advice feel emotionally wrong?
- What should we stop expecting from each other?
- How can we protect the friendship as life changes?

### Future
- Can this friendship stay meaningful through change?
- What will help it adapt?
- What pressure points should we watch?
- How do we preserve closeness without demanding constant availability?

The product may discuss adaptability and pressure carefully.

It must not predict a friendship lifespan or specific separation event.

---

## 4A.2 Priority user jobs

| Priority | User job | Required answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | “이 친구는 내 삶에서 어떤 사람이지?” | A clear, flexible friendship role grounded in evidence—not a fixed label. |
| 2 | “우리는 서로에게 뭘 주지?” | A directional A→B and B→A gift exchange with distinct meaning. |
| 3 | “언제 이 우정이 가장 빛나지?” | Supported situations where the relationship’s strengths are most useful. |
| 4 | “왜 가끔 힘들지?” | The mechanism that turns a gift into a cost under the wrong condition. |
| 5 | “어떻게 더 잘 지낼 수 있지?” | Specific requests, expectations, and care moves shaped by the pair. |

The first mobile experience must answer jobs 1–3 quickly enough that the user feels immediate value.

# 5. Emotional Journey

## 5.1 Intended emotional curve

The experience should move through:

**Curiosity → Recognition → Appreciation → Surprise → Understanding → Relief → Agency → Warmth**

### Curiosity
Make the user want to know what role the friend truly plays.

### Recognition
Show a scene or situation that feels immediately familiar.

### Appreciation
Help the user see what the friend contributes without sentimental exaggeration.

### Surprise
Reveal a non-obvious relationship-only effect or reversal.

### Understanding
Explain why a valued strength may also create friction.

### Relief
Reduce blame by showing context, timing, and expectation mismatch.

### Agency
Offer a better way to ask, respond, invite, or set limits.

### Warmth
End with a believable sense of gratitude, clarity, and relational possibility.

---

## 5.2 Engagement principle

Each module should create a question that the next module answers.

Examples:

- “This friend stabilizes you.” → “When is that steadiness most useful?”
- “They solve problems quickly.” → “Why can that same strength feel cold?”
- “You bring courage into their life.” → “What becomes possible in them around you?”
- “You are different socially.” → “How do you enjoy each other without draining one person?”
- “You both care.” → “How do you show it in forms the other person can actually receive?”

Transitions must create forward pull.

They must not feel like unrelated cards.

---

## 5.3 Re-read value

High re-read assets include:

- friendship role summary,
- “when this friendship shines” scenes,
- gift-to-cost translation,
- support request language,
- expectation reset,
- do / do not guidance,
- and “who we become together.”

A successful Friend report should function both as a first-time emotional experience and as a practical friendship reference later.

# 6. Hero Experience

## 6.1 Purpose

The Hero must establish the friendship’s relational identity without evaluating it.

The Hero should make the user think:

> “맞아, 이 친구랑 있으면 딱 이래.”

not:

> “우리 우정 점수는 높은가?”

---

## 6.2 Hero content

The Hero should contain:

1. **The two names**
2. **A friendship signature**
3. **A one-sentence relational truth**
4. **A central gift or paradox**
5. **An invitation into the journey**

Optional:

- a primary role,
- a secondary role,
- one supported situational cue,
- a concise Saju-informed identity line,
- or a relationship motif that does not behave like a score.

---

## 6.3 Friendship signature

The signature is not a grade and not a poetic elemental metaphor.

It is a concise interpretation of what these two people create together.

Acceptable forms:

- “힘들 때는 중심을 잡아주고, 평소에는 서로의 세상을 넓혀주는 우정”
- “한 사람은 가능성을 열고, 한 사람은 그것을 현실로 이어주는 우정”
- “연락의 빈도보다 필요할 때의 존재감이 더 큰 우정”
- “같이 있으면 한 사람은 더 용감해지고, 다른 사람은 더 가벼워지는 우정”

Unacceptable forms:

- “불과 흙의 완벽한 우정”
- “평생 베스트 프렌드”
- “95점짜리 우정”
- “절대 배신하지 않는 친구”
- “20대에만 빛나는 친구”

---

## 6.4 Role architecture

A friendship may have:

- one **Primary Role**,
- one **Secondary Role**,
- and optional minor situational roles.

Example:

```text
Primary: Safe Harbor
Secondary: Reality Anchor
Situational: Growth Catalyst
```

Role rules:

- roles summarize repeated relational functions,
- roles must be evidence-grounded,
- roles must not become moral rankings,
- roles must not become obligations,
- and roles must remain open to context.

---

# 7. Core Experience Architecture

The final experience is organized as modules, not numbered report parts.

## Module 1 — Opening Scene: “This is your friendship”

Purpose:

- immediate recognition,
- establish the friendship signature,
- name the central relational gift,
- create curiosity.

Primary assets:

- Hero,
- friendship signature,
- one recognizable scene,
- primary and secondary role,
- optional signature chips.

Success test:

The user can explain in one sentence why this friend matters.

---

## Module 2 — What This Friend Brings You

Purpose:

- explain the friend’s directional contribution,
- translate traits into lived support,
- show how the support feels in real life.

Primary assets:

- A→B gift,
- primary role,
- secondary role,
- emotional support style,
- practical support style,
- energy and perspective contribution.

Required structure:

1. What they give
2. How it feels
3. When it matters
4. What not to overexpect

Example:

> “When everything feels too heavy to explain, this friend may not always have the perfect words. Their strength is that they do not disappear easily. They can become the person who stays beside you until the situation feels survivable again.”

Success test:

The user recognizes at least one real moment when they would naturally turn to this friend.

---

## Module 3 — What You Bring Them

Purpose:

- explain the user’s directional contribution,
- preserve mutuality,
- show what may become more available in the friend through this relationship.

Primary assets:

- B→A gift,
- support contribution,
- energy contribution,
- permission or activation,
- relationship-shaped change.

Required causal form:

```text
What you bring
  → What the friend receives
  → What may become easier in them
```

Example:

> “You bring movement and possibility into this friend’s life. Around you, a person who normally waits until everything is certain may find it easier to try first and adjust later.”

Success test:

The user sees their own meaningful place in the friendship without receiving generic praise.

---

## Module 4 — Where This Friendship Shines

Purpose:

- identify the situations where the pair’s strengths are most useful,
- turn abstract strengths into recognizable scenes,
- help the user know when to lean into the friendship.

Possible situation families:

| Situation family | Human meaning |
|---|---|
| **Emotional storm** | When one person feels overwhelmed, hurt, lonely, or ready to cry |
| **Reality check** | When a difficult practical decision must be made |
| **New beginning** | When courage, novelty, or movement is needed |
| **Long road** | When consistency and patience matter |
| **Quiet recovery** | When stimulation should decrease and safe presence matters |
| **Recognition moment** | When effort feels unseen or confidence is low |
| **Social expansion** | When new people, ideas, or experiences are needed |
| **Deep conversation** | When complexity, honesty, and perspective matter |
| **Play and release** | When life needs lightness, humor, or shared enjoyment |

Rules:

- choose only situations supported by evidence,
- prioritize the top two or three,
- do not fabricate precise ratings,
- do not turn situational fit into a life-stage forecast,
- and show what each person contributes in the scene.

Success test:

The user can answer:

> “이 친구는 특히 이런 날 생각나는 사람이야.”

---

## Module 5 — When the Gift Becomes a Cost

Purpose:

- show how valued strengths can become frustrating under the wrong conditions,
- reduce blame,
- explain mismatch in timing, form, or expectation.

Primary assets:

- gift-to-cost conversion,
- energy mismatch,
- practical-versus-emotional mismatch,
- social pace,
- planning versus spontaneity,
- initiation imbalance,
- overreliance on one role.

Preferred form:

| Strength | Helpful when | Difficult when | Better interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Practicality | A decision must be made | The user needs emotional validation first | The friend may be helping in the wrong order, not caring less |
| Calmness | The situation is chaotic | Urgency or visible reassurance is needed | Stability may look like emotional distance |
| Stimulation | Life feels stagnant | One person is depleted | Excitement may become pressure |
| Structure | A long plan is needed | Rapid adaptation is required | Reliability may feel inflexible |

Success test:

The user understands one recurring frustration without invalidating the underlying strength.

---

## Module 6 — Who You Become Together

Purpose:

- identify the relationship-only version of both people,
- explain what emerges only in the pair,
- create a distinct friendship identity.

Primary assets:

- mutual activation,
- shared energy,
- pair-only behavior,
- joint courage,
- calm,
- playfulness,
- depth,
- discipline,
- openness,
- or adaptability.

Rules:

- synthesize at least two directional findings,
- do not merely restate Module 2 and Module 3,
- focus on the joint system,
- use tendency language,
- and avoid implying that the user cannot access these qualities without the friend.

Examples:

- “Together, the two of you may become more adventurous than either person is alone.”
- “This friendship can create a quieter, more honest version of both of you.”
- “One person opens possibilities while the other prevents them from dissolving into ideas.”

Success test:

The user recognizes a version of themselves that appears specifically in this friendship.

---

## Module 7 — How to Bring Out the Best in Each Other

Purpose:

- convert analysis into practical friendship care,
- show how to invite strengths rather than demand them,
- reset unrealistic expectations.

Primary assets:

- support request language,
- timing,
- recognition preferences,
- invitation style,
- limits,
- expectation reset,
- do / do not.

Recommended action types:

| Type | Product job |
|---|---|
| **Ask clearly** | Make the needed support form explicit |
| **Time it well** | Match conversation or action timing |
| **Recognize** | Acknowledge the contribution the friend naturally values |
| **Give space** | Prevent closeness from becoming pressure |
| **Invite** | Create the environment where a strength can appear |
| **Reset expectation** | Stop expecting one friend to meet every need |
| **Protect** | Preserve a strength already helping the friendship |

Example request:

> “오늘은 해결책보다 그냥 내 얘기를 들어줬으면 좋겠어.”

This works when the friend’s strength is practical help but the user currently needs emotional presence.

Success test:

The user can choose one specific friendship behavior to try.

---

## Module 8 — Friendship Care Guide

Purpose:

- make the report useful during ordinary strain,
- provide a small set of re-readable guidance,
- preserve the friendship without forcing constant closeness.

Primary assets:

- what to continue,
- what to stop assuming,
- how to repair after disappointment,
- what form of contact works,
- what pressure to avoid,
- one sentence to try,
- one small ritual or habit.

Rules:

- keep the action set small,
- connect every recommendation to earlier evidence,
- do not prescribe constant availability,
- do not frame distance as failure,
- and do not make one friend responsible for the other’s emotional regulation.

Success test:

The user leaves with clarity rather than a long checklist.

---

## Module 9 — Friendship Horizon

Purpose:

- provide a careful, future-facing synthesis,
- explain what helps the friendship adapt through change,
- end with believable possibility.

May discuss:

- how the bond handles distance,
- whether it relies on frequent contact or dependable presence,
- what may happen when responsibilities increase,
- which expectation requires flexibility,
- and what quality is worth preserving.

Must not discuss:

- exact friendship lifespan,
- the decade in which the friendship will end,
- a guaranteed future event,
- betrayal prediction,
- or deterministic “friendship season.”

Preferred framing:

```text
When unexamined
  → the strength may harden into expectation or role imbalance.

When understood
  → the pair can use the strength more flexibly and protect the friendship through change.
```

Success test:

The user leaves feeling clearer and more respectful of the friendship’s real shape.

---

## 7.1 Deep Read

Deep Read rewards users who want richer understanding beneath the visible friendship.

It may deepen:

- individual friendship nature,
- directional gifts,
- role flexibility,
- situational strengths,
- hidden costs,
- pair-only behavior,
- social versus private dynamics,
- and long-form synthesis.

Deep Read must deepen the report, not restart it.

It must not repeat visible cards in paragraph form.

---

## 7.2 Save, Share, and Revisit

Potential experiences:

- a shareable friendship signature,
- saved “when this friendship shines” cards,
- selected support-request scripts,
- a friend-facing summary,
- revisitable friendship care guide,
- and future comparison after updated psychological input.

Sharing must preserve dignity.

Never encourage users to weaponize a role, weakness, score, or prediction against a friend.

---

# 8. Situation Architecture

## 8.1 Why situations matter

Traits alone do not explain friendship value.

The same friend may be:

- excellent during crisis,
- average at spontaneous adventure,
- strong for long-term planning,
- and difficult when the user wants immediate emotional reassurance.

The product must therefore map strength to context.

---

## 8.2 Situation evidence principle

A situation may be surfaced when supported by a combination of:

- relevant 11-axis strength,
- pair difference or complementarity,
- Saju relationship direction,
- pair-specific relational signals,
- and sufficient evidence confidence.

No one axis should automatically create a full situation claim.

---

## 8.3 Illustrative situation mappings

These are product-level interpretive families, not final technical formulas.

| Evidence family | Possible situation meaning |
|---|---|
| empathy + resilience | emotional crisis, grief, difficult recovery |
| structure + self_control | long-term project, stability, disciplined follow-through |
| practicality + decision_style | reality check, concrete decision, next-step planning |
| stimulation + energy_style | new experiences, movement, social expansion |
| recognition + empathy | confidence loss, feeling unseen, emotional encouragement |
| thinking_style + conflict_style | complex problem, perspective, difficult conversation |
| adaptability + resilience | sudden change, uncertainty, transition |
| low stimulation + strong empathy | quiet recovery, low-pressure companionship |

Final mappings belong in `06B_Friend_Technical_Blueprint.md`.

---

## 8.4 Situation selection rule

The report should normally surface:

- two or three strongest supported situations,
- one carefully framed cost situation,
- and one practical way to invite the friendship’s strength.

Do not display a false precision matrix merely because many categories are available.

---

## 8.5 Situation copy contract

Every situation insight should answer:

1. What is happening?
2. Why might this friendship help?
3. What does each person contribute?
4. What limitation or condition matters?
5. How can the user invite the strength?

---

# 9. Role Architecture

## 9.1 Purpose

Roles help users remember what a friend tends to bring.

Roles are summary devices—not identity verdicts.

---

## 9.2 Candidate role families

Possible role families include:

- **Safe Harbor** — steady emotional presence,
- **Reality Anchor** — practical clarity and grounded decisions,
- **Growth Catalyst** — challenge, movement, and new possibilities,
- **Joy Companion** — play, humor, lightness, and shared release,
- **Perspective Giver** — reframing, complexity, and alternative viewpoints,
- **Steady Witness** — continuity, loyalty, and remembering the user over time,
- **Quiet Protector** — support through presence and action rather than display,
- **Connector** — people, experiences, opportunities, and social expansion,
- **Confidence Builder** — recognition, encouragement, and visible belief,
- **Co-Creator** — shared ideas, plans, or meaningful projects.

These labels are examples of a role system.

Final canonical enums and thresholds belong in `06B`.

---

## 9.3 Role selection rules

- Prefer one primary and one secondary role.
- Add a minor situational role only when useful.
- Do not force a role when evidence is weak.
- Do not use roles to rank friendships.
- Do not imply role exclusivity.
- Do not assign moral superiority.
- Do not expose raw technical role-selection logic as the main experience.

---

## 9.4 Role copy pattern

Preferred:

> “이 친구는 당신이 흔들릴 때 감정을 크게 끌어올리기보다, 자리를 지키고 현실을 정리해주는 쪽에 가깝습니다. 그래서 힘든 순간에는 ‘Safe Harbor’와 ‘Reality Anchor’ 역할이 함께 나타날 수 있습니다.”

Avoid:

> “이 친구는 무조건 당신의 평생 보호자입니다.”

---

# 10. Storytelling Rules

## 10.1 The narrative unit

Every meaningful insight should follow this pattern:

1. **Recognizable situation**
2. **What one person contributes**
3. **What the other person receives**
4. **What happens between them**
5. **Supporting evidence**
6. **What changes the outcome**

Not every card needs all six visibly, but the product logic should contain them.

---

## 10.2 Scene before label

Bad:

> “A is high in structure and B is high in stimulation.”

Better:

> “한 사람은 계획이 있어야 마음이 놓이고, 다른 사람은 일단 움직여야 재미와 가능성이 생깁니다. 함께하면 시작은 빨라지고 실행은 오래갈 수 있지만, 준비 속도를 두고 서로를 답답하게 느낄 수도 있습니다.”

Then show evidence.

---

## 10.3 Human language before system language

Prefer:

- “힘들 때 곁을 지키는 힘”
- “현실적인 다음 단계를 찾는 방식”
- “새로운 경험을 열어주는 에너지”
- “마음을 알아차리고 확인해주는 방식”
- “조용히 회복할 수 있는 관계”

over raw enum names or technical axis labels.

System terms may appear in expandable evidence details.

---

## 10.4 No excerpt rewriting

LLM narrative must not simply paraphrase one evidence field.

A strong insight should synthesize at least two meaningful sources whenever possible.

Examples:

- empathy + resilience,
- practicality + decision style,
- stimulation + energy style,
- recognition + relationship role,
- day-stem direction + 11-axis refinement,
- role evidence + situational evidence.

---

## 10.5 Surprise without invention

Valid surprise:

> “평소에는 현실적인 말을 먼저 하는 이 친구가, 실제로는 당신이 무너지지 않도록 상황을 붙잡아주려는 쪽일 수 있습니다.”

Invalid surprise:

> “이 친구는 어린 시절 상처 때문에 감정을 피합니다.”

unless such information is explicitly known and appropriate—which is outside standard product scope.

---

## 10.6 One voice, multiple registers

The product has one consistent narrator:

- warm,
- perceptive,
- concise,
- emotionally intelligent,
- lightly playful when appropriate,
- never clinical,
- never mystical,
- never patronizing.

Registers may shift:

- Hero: warm and memorable,
- Gift: appreciative,
- Situation: concrete and cinematic,
- Cost: precise and fair,
- Shared identity: surprising and relational,
- Guide: practical,
- Closing: warm and realistic.

---

# 11. Copy Principles

## 11.1 Tone

The tone should feel like an insightful friend who knows that friendships are valuable, imperfect, and context-dependent.

It should be:

- warm,
- elegant,
- intelligent,
- direct,
- specific,
- and humane.

---

## 11.2 Address law

Use names whenever possible.

Avoid anonymous “Person A / Person B” in narrative copy.

Avoid “너” except inside carefully constructed dialogue quotes.

Use natural role descriptions where helpful:

- 먼저 연락하는 쪽,
- 현실적인 해결책을 찾는 쪽,
- 감정을 먼저 알아차리는 쪽,
- 새로운 경험을 제안하는 쪽,
- 조용히 곁을 지키는 쪽.

---

## 11.3 Tendency, not verdict

Preferred:

- “~하는 편입니다”
- “이런 상황에서는 ~가 될 수 있습니다”
- “두 사람 사이에서는 ~로 나타나기 쉽습니다”
- “압박이 커질수록 ~하게 느껴질 수 있습니다”

Avoid:

- “항상 이런 사람입니다”
- “평생 당신 곁에 있습니다”
- “절대 배신하지 않습니다”
- “이 우정은 끝납니다”

---

## 11.4 Capability, not obligation

Preferred:

> “이 친구는 힘든 순간 든든한 내 편이 될 수 있습니다.”

Avoid:

> “이 친구는 언제나 당신을 책임져야 합니다.”

---

## 11.5 No generic praise symmetry

Do not force equal compliments.

Do not write:

> “Both of you are amazing friends in different ways.”

unless the statement carries specific pair meaning.

---

## 11.6 Specificity test

Before approving copy, ask:

> Could this sentence apply unchanged to most friendships?

If yes, it is too generic.

---

## 11.7 Humor rule

Humor may create recognition and lower defensiveness.

It must never:

- mock loneliness,
- shame emotional need,
- trivialize betrayal or serious hurt,
- or reduce one friend to a punchline.

---

# 12. Visual Principles

## 12.1 Visual hierarchy

Meaning first, situation second, evidence third, detail fourth.

Each module should prioritize:

1. friendship insight,
2. recognizable moment,
3. directional contribution,
4. evidence,
5. optional depth.

---

## 12.2 No dashboard-first design

The product must not look like a friendship analytics dashboard.

Charts and tables support the story.

They do not become the story.

---

## 12.3 Use visual form by communication job

### Narrative card
Use for:

- friendship signature,
- primary gift,
- emotional reframe.

### Directional pair card
Use for:

- what they bring you,
- what you bring them.

### Situation card
Use for:

- where the friendship shines,
- scene-based strength.

### Gift-to-cost table
Use for:

- helpful versus difficult context,

### Radar
Use for:

- broad pair shape,

### Role map
Use for:

- primary and secondary roles,

### Dialogue block

- repair,

### Expandable deep read
Use for:

- nuance,

---

## 12.4 Density rhythm

Alternate between:

- emotionally rich text,
- quick visual comprehension,
- concrete situations,
- and actionable summary.

Avoid long uninterrupted essays.

Avoid endless role cards.

Avoid displaying every possible situation.

# 13. Evidence Principles

## 13.1 Evidence source hierarchy

Potential evidence sources include:

- pair Saju interactions,
- day-stem interaction,
- combined element notes,
- relationship-specific Saju signals,
- 11-axis psychological data,
- absolute axis strengths,
- pair differences,
- complementarity,
- contextual outputs,
- deterministic resolver judgments,
- corroborating cross-hits,
- and approved pair dynamics.

The final technical mapping belongs in `06B_Friend_Technical_Blueprint.md`.

---

## 13.2 Evidence roles

### Saju
Provides relational direction, role tendency, symbolic structure, and pair-specific interaction context.

### 11-axis
Refines observable behavior, support style, timing, energy, recognition, decision, and conflict expression.

### Pair comparison
Shows similarity, difference, complementarity, and likely translation needs.

### Relationship context
Determines which interpretation matters specifically for friendship.

### LLM
Translates approved evidence into human narrative.

The LLM does not create canonical truth.

---

## 13.3 Evidence tier

### Corroborated
Multiple approved sources point in the same direction.

Tone may be direct.

### Refined
The direction is supported, but context or conditions matter.

Tone should be specific and conditional.

### Tentative
Evidence is limited or mixed.

Tone should clearly signal possibility.

### Unsupported
Do not show.

---

## 13.4 Severity is separate from confidence

A friendship cost may be highly supported or only tentative.

A strength may also be highly supported.

Do not confuse evidence quality with relational seriousness.

Suggested internal ladder:

- **S0 Strength**
- **S1 Difference**
- **S2 Friction**
- **S3 Strain**
- **S4 Boundary / Safety Concern**

The Friend product should rarely expose these labels directly.

Ordinary friendship logic must not be used to normalize serious harm.

---

## 13.5 Directionality

A→B and B→A must remain distinct.

The system must preserve:

- who gives,
- who receives,
- what changes,
- and whose evidence owns the direction.

Symmetric evidence may create a shared insight.

Directional evidence must not be rewritten as symmetric merely for visual neatness.

---

# 14. Free and Premium Strategy

Premium must not mean “more text.”

## 14.1 Free experience

The free Friend experience should establish trust and feel useful.

It should answer:

- the overall friendship signature,
- one important role,
- one directional gift,
- one situation where the friendship shines,
- and one preview of deeper mutual interpretation.

---

## 14.2 Premium experience

Premium value comes from deeper synthesis, directionality, context, and action:

- full A→B and B→A gift exchange,
- primary and secondary roles,
- situation architecture,
- gift-to-cost conversion,
- who the pair becomes together,
- support and request language,
- friendship care guide,
- deeper evidence explanation,
- and a careful future adaptation view.

### Premium promise

> Free tells users what the friendship feels like. Premium explains what each person brings, when it matters most, where it becomes difficult, and how to use the relationship more wisely.

---

## 14.3 What must feel premium

- specificity,
- directional accuracy,
- emotional recognition,
- situational usefulness,
- multi-source synthesis,
- balanced dignity,
- low repetition,
- elegant pacing,
- and practical friendship care.

Length alone is not premium.

Role labels alone are not premium.

Charts alone are not premium.

---

# 15. Product Success Criteria

## 15.1 Memory outcomes

A world-class Friend report should leave users remembering:

1. **Our friendship signature**
2. **What this friend brings me**
3. **What I bring this friend**
4. **When this friendship shines**
5. **When the same strength becomes difficult**
6. **Who we become together**
7. **One useful way to care for the friendship**

If users remember many labels but cannot explain these, the product has failed.

---

## 15.2 User success

The report succeeds when users can answer:

- Why does this friend matter to me?
- What kind of support do they naturally give?
- What do I give them?
- When should I naturally lean into this friendship?
- Where do we misread each other?
- What should I ask for more clearly?
- What expectation should I loosen?
- What version of us appears together?

Behavioral signals of success include:

- recognizing a real friendship scene,
- sharing a section with the friend,
- appreciating an overlooked contribution,
- reinterpreting a frustrating habit,
- using a suggested sentence,
- adjusting an expectation,
- or revisiting the report during a meaningful moment.

---

## 15.3 Experience success

- The friendship’s central meaning is recognizable within the first mobile scroll.
- The user understands the relationship before seeing technical evidence.
- A→B and B→A remain distinct.
- Strength is attached to situation.
- Cost is explained as context-dependent.
- Situation is not inflated into life-stage prophecy.
- No friend is reduced to one permanent role.
- Every difficult insight includes a usable path.
- The experience feels like one story, not a pile of generated cards.
- Both people retain dignity.
- Premium value is visible through synthesis and usefulness.

---

## 15.4 Business success

- Free users understand the Friend product’s unique value.
- Premium value comes from directionality, situations, and action.
- The report creates natural save, share, and revisit behavior.
- Friend establishes a reusable relationship interpretation model without forcing Romantic, Work, or Family into Friend logic.
- Product meaning remains stable even if implementation changes.

---

## 15.5 Module-level quality checks

Every major module must pass:

### Human question check
Does it answer something a real friend would want to understand?

### Recognition check
Does it contain a concrete scene, situation, or behavior?

### Direction check
Is it clear who brings what to whom?

### Situation check
Is the strength attached to a meaningful context?

### Evidence check
Is the claim supported?

### Duplication check
Does this module add new meaning?

### Boundary check
Does it avoid obligation, ranking, prediction, or product leakage?

### Action check
Does the user know what to do with the insight?

### Tone check
Does it sound warm, specific, and non-judgmental?

# 16. Do / Don’t Constitution

## Do

- lead with human meaning,
- use names,
- show situations,
- preserve directionality,
- synthesize Saju and 11-axis evidence,
- show what each person brings,
- attach strengths to moments,
- show when gifts become costs,
- make roles flexible,
- offer realistic request language,
- and end with warmth and agency.

## Don’t

- rank friends,
- declare a “best friend,”
- lead with scores,
- predict friendship lifespan,
- create unsupported Friendship Seasons,
- use one axis as the whole story,
- force equal praise,
- turn roles into obligations,
- invent loyalty or betrayal claims,
- repeat A→B gifts as a separate transformation section,
- use shallow elemental metaphors,
- or give generic advice unrelated to evidence.

# 17. Product Decisions Locked by This Document

The following decisions are locked unless a future product decision explicitly supersedes them:

1. Friend is a story-led friendship experience, not a compatibility report.
2. The friendship—not either individual—is the protagonist.
3. The product answers what each person brings, when it matters, where it becomes costly, and how to care for the bond.
4. A→B and B→A are directional and must remain distinct.
5. “What I bring” and “who the friend becomes with me” belong in one causal flow to avoid duplication.
6. Strengths must be attached to real situations.
7. `Where This Friendship Shines` is canonical.
8. Time-based `Friendship Season` prediction is not part of the current product.
9. Situational fit must not become a life-stage or lifespan prediction.
10. Roles may be primary, secondary, and situational; no person is reduced to one label.
11. The same trait may be a gift or a cost depending on context.
12. Saju provides relational direction; 11-axis evidence refines behavioral manifestation.
13. No single signal owns the story.
14. LLM prose never creates canonical relationship truth.
15. Advice follows recognition and explanation.
16. Capability must never be written as obligation.
17. No grade, rank, loyalty verdict, betrayal prediction, or fate claim may replace understanding.
18. Premium value comes from synthesis, situation, directionality, and action—not length.
19. Friend remains separate from Romantic, Work, and Family ownership.
20. This document (`05B`) is the sole Friend Product SSOT.

# 20. Final Principle

The user should not leave thinking about axes, elements, scores, prompts, or evidence tiers.

> “이 친구가 왜 내 삶에서 중요한지 알겠다.”

> **what this friendship gives, when it matters, what it costs, and who both people can become together.**
