# 05E Partnership Product Blueprint

> **Product scope:** Marriage / Cohabitation / Long-term Committed Partnership  
> **Document role:** Product-level SSOT for the Partnership relationship analysis  
> **Status:** Draft v1 — product-specific blueprint before cross-product refactor  
> **Primary question:** **Can these two people build, operate, repair, and grow a shared life together?**

---

## 0. Document Intent

This document defines the Partnership product only.

It does not attempt to become the final cross-product Relationship Intelligence Bible. Shared rules such as evidence policy, confidence language, safety, narrative voice, LLM behavior, and platform-wide UX may later be extracted after Romantic, Friend, Work, Family, and Partnership are reviewed together.

For now, this document preserves enough local guidance to make Partnership coherent and implementable.

### Included relationships

- Married couples
- Long-term partners
- Cohabiting partners
- Engaged couples preparing for shared life
- Couples planning a durable household, family, or financial future

### Excluded relationships

- Early dating without shared-life intent
- Casual relationships
- Pure attraction or chemistry analysis
- Abuse screening or clinical diagnosis
- Divorce prediction
- Fertility, illness, or child-future prediction

# PART I. Why Partnership Exists

## 1. Romantic Is Not Enough

Romantic analysis asks:

> How do these two people love, desire, reassure, misunderstand, and reconnect?

Partnership asks:

> What happens when love must survive schedules, money, chores, careers, parents, fatigue, children, decisions, crises, and time?

A couple may love each other deeply and still struggle to operate a life together. Another couple may appear highly practical but feel emotionally abandoned inside the system they built.

Partnership exists to explain the bridge between:

```text
Love
↓
Daily Life
↓
Shared Responsibility
↓
Conflict and Repair
↓
Future
```

The product does not replace Romantic. It inherits the emotional truth of Romantic and reinterprets it under the pressure of real life.

---

## 2. Core Product Philosophy

> **Love begins as a feeling, but partnership becomes visible through repeated choices.**

A committed relationship is not only an emotional bond. It is also a shared system made of:

- attention
- labor
- reliability
- money
- decisions
- boundaries
- recovery
- future direction

The product must never reduce the couple to household efficiency. Love remains present throughout the report.

The goal is not to judge whether the relationship is “good” or “bad.”

The goal is to translate:

- what each person contributes
- what each person experiences
- where love becomes distorted by logistics
- what strength becomes costly under stress
- which conflicts are negotiable
- which differences require explicit life decisions
- what helps the couple return to each other

---

## 3. Product Promise

Partnership does not say:

- You are destined to stay together.
- You are incompatible.
- This marriage will fail.
- One person is the problem.
- A specific future event will occur.

Partnership says:

- This is how your shared life may naturally organize itself.
- This is what each person may mistake about the other.
- This is where invisible burden can accumulate.
- This is what the relationship gains from each person.
- This is what needs conversation rather than prediction.
- This is how love can become more recognizable in daily life.

> **We do not decide the future of the relationship. We make the relationship easier to understand.**

---

# PART II. Product Identity

## 4. The Product’s Central Question

The Partnership product answers five connected questions:

1. **Why did these two people choose each other?**
2. **Who do they become when they build a life together?**
3. **How does their shared life actually run?**
4. **Where does love get lost inside responsibility and stress?**
5. **What helps them keep choosing each other over time?**

---

## 5. Distinction from Other Relationship Products

### Romantic

Focus:

- attraction
- emotional intimacy
- reassurance
- affection
- conflict chemistry
- romantic repair

Partnership reinterpretation:

- how affection becomes care
- how reassurance becomes reliability
- how attraction survives fatigue and routine
- how love language becomes labor, money, and decisions

### Family

Focus:

- generational roles
- parent-child dynamics
- family climate
- guidance, recognition, and distance

Partnership distinction:

- two adults building a shared unit
- negotiation between equals
- household, finance, life direction, and mutual responsibility

### Friend

Focus:

- companionship
- reciprocity
- trust
- social rhythm

Partnership distinction:

- deeper dependency
- higher operational stakes
- long-term shared consequences

### Work

Focus:

- execution
- collaboration
- feedback
- authority

Partnership distinction:

- emotional attachment and private life
- care obligations
- shared future and identity
- love cannot be treated as performance management

# PART III. Evidence and Interpretation

## 6. Evidence Hierarchy

Partnership uses three major layers:

```text
Current Behavior Psychology
↓
Relationship Interaction
↓
Underlying Tendency Signals
```

### 6.1 Current behavior psychology owns present behavior

The 11 behavioral axes describe how the person currently tends to act, decide, regulate, connect, and respond.

The behavioral layer takes priority when it conflicts with underlying tendency signals.

### 6.2 Relationship interaction owns the couple pattern

A relationship pattern is not simply Person A plus Person B.

It emerges from interaction:

- A’s behavior triggers B’s interpretation
- B’s response changes A’s state
- repeated states become a couple loop

### 6.3 Underlying tendency signals are explanatory, never user-facing jargon

Any traditional interpretive source may be used only as an internal tendency hypothesis.

It must never be exposed through technical fortune-telling vocabulary.

Forbidden in user-facing output:

- traditional technical terms
- elemental language
- fate claims
- symbolic jargon
- deterministic compatibility labels

Required transformation:

```text
Internal Signal
↓
Behavioral Hypothesis
↓
11-Axis Confirmation or Correction
↓
Real-Life Pattern
↓
Partner Experience
↓
Strength / Cost / Adjustment
```

Example:

**Forbidden**

> One partner’s symbolic energy generates the other’s element.

**Required**

> One person naturally brings momentum and emotional color, while the other gives that energy structure and continuity. Together, they can turn possibility into something stable.

---

## 7. The 11 Behavioral Axes in Partnership

The 11 axes are not displayed as isolated personality scores. They become evidence for specific shared-life questions.

| Axis | Partnership relevance |
|---|---|
| `stimulation` | novelty, boredom, leisure rhythm, appetite for change |
| `self_control` | follow-through, impulse regulation, conflict containment, spending restraint |
| `practicality` | execution, resource use, household realism, concrete care |
| `structure` | routines, standards, planning, predictability |
| `empathy` | emotional recognition, perspective-taking, comfort response |
| `conflict_style` | pursuit, withdrawal, directness, escalation pattern |
| `resilience` | recovery after stress, setbacks, conflict persistence |
| `recognition` | need for appreciation, visibility of labor, respect signals |
| `energy_style` | social and physical energy, rest needs, pace of life |
| `thinking_style` | detail vs big picture, linear vs associative processing |
| `decision_style` | speed, certainty, consultation, risk, ownership |

### 7.1 Axis use rule

No module should merely restate an axis.

Each module must convert axes into:

- observable scene
- likely interpretation
- relational benefit
- system cost
- action or conversation

# PART IV. Core Narrative Architecture

## 8. Final Narrative Journey

The Partnership report follows this emotional arc:

1. **Why You Chose Each Other**
2. **Who You Become Together**
3. **How You Run a Real Life**
4. **Where Love Gets Lost in Logistics**
5. **Remember This Person**
6. **The Life You Are Building**
7. **How You Repair and Grow**
8. **Why This Is Still Your Person**

The report begins with love, enters reality, confronts friction, restores perspective, and closes with agency rather than prediction.

---

## 9. Chapter 1 — Why You Chose Each Other

### Product purpose

Reconnect the couple to the original attraction without repeating the Romantic report.

### Questions answered

- What did each person notice first?
- What emotional need did the relationship meet?
- What did one person awaken or stabilize in the other?
- What still remains true beneath current stress?

### Required structure

```text
Initial Attraction
↓
Emotional Gift
↓
Daily-Life Translation
↓
What Can Be Forgotten Under Stress
```

### Example direction

> You may have first been drawn to the ease, warmth, steadiness, courage, or aliveness this person brought into your life. In a long-term partnership, that same quality becomes practical: it shapes how the home feels, how crises are handled, and how each of you returns to yourselves.

### Guardrail

Do not imply that attraction proves long-term compatibility.

---

## 10. Chapter 2 — Who You Become Together

### Product purpose

Explain the couple as a third system, not merely two separate personalities.

### Questions answered

- What does this couple do better together?
- Who brings momentum?
- Who brings continuity?
- Who opens possibility?
- Who protects stability?
- What role does each person naturally fall into?
- Are these roles chosen, assumed, or silently imposed?

### Core model

```text
Person A Strength
+
Person B Strength
=
Couple Capability
```

But also:

```text
Person A Overfunction
+
Person B Underfunction
=
Role Trap
```

### Required distinction

A complementary role is not automatically healthy.

The report must distinguish:

- healthy complement
- invisible dependence
- unequal burden
- unrecognized labor
- rigid role assignment

### Gender neutrality

Never assign roles by husband/wife, man/woman, or traditional family expectations.

---

# PART V. Core Modules

## 12. Reliability and Follow-Through

### Core question

> Can each person trust the other to carry what they said they would carry?

### Evidence

- self_control
- practicality
- structure
- resilience
- decision_style

### What to analyze

- promise-to-action consistency
- planning versus improvisation
- crisis reliability versus daily reliability
- unfinished tasks
- forgotten agreements
- overpromising
- rescuing behavior
- whether one partner becomes the default backup system

### Important distinction

A person may be highly reliable in crisis but inconsistent in routine.

Another may be excellent at daily continuity but freeze during sudden change.

Both forms matter and should not be collapsed into one score.

### User-facing frame

> The issue is not simply who is more responsible. It is whether both partners can predict how shared obligations will be carried without one person constantly monitoring the other.

---

## 13. Household Rhythm

### Core question

> How do these two people define “a home that works”?

### Evidence

- structure
- practicality
- energy_style
- self_control
- thinking_style

### What to analyze

- cleanliness threshold
- timing and routines
- standards versus flexibility
- visible tasks versus background maintenance
- speed versus thoroughness
- task ownership
- definition of “done”

### Conflict map

```text
Different Standard
↓
One Person Acts First
↓
The Other Assumes It Is Handled
↓
Resentment Grows
↓
The Issue Becomes Respect, Not Cleaning
```

### Product rule

Do not recommend a perfectly equal 50/50 split by default.

Aim for:

- clarity
- fairness
- ownership
- consent
- recognition

---

## 14. Mental Load and Invisible Work

### Core question

> Who remembers, anticipates, notices, schedules, reminds, and emotionally carries the household?

### Evidence

- structure
- thinking_style
- practicality
- empathy
- recognition
- self_control

### What to analyze

- remembering appointments
- anticipating shortages or deadlines
- planning social and family obligations
- noticing emotional atmosphere
- initiating difficult conversations
- tracking children, parents, health, school, or logistics
- whether one partner manages both the task and the other person

### Critical distinction

Doing a task is not the same as owning it.

Ownership includes:

```text
Notice
↓
Plan
↓
Initiate
↓
Complete
↓
Follow Up
```

### User-facing frame

> The heaviest burden is often not the visible task. It is being the person who must keep the task alive in their mind.

### Safety

Never treat high mental load as a personality flaw.

## 16. Decision-Making and Power

### Core question

> How do decisions get made, and whose reality carries more weight?

### Evidence

- decision_style
- self_control
- thinking_style
- empathy
- recognition
- conflict_style

### What to analyze

- speed of decision
- consultation need
- confidence and certainty
- veto power
- financial authority
- emotional authority
- expertise-based leadership
- default decision maker
- whether silence is mistaken for agreement

### Power distinction

Power is not only who speaks louder.

- social energy

# PART VI. Where Love Gets Lost

## 22. Love Translation Gap

### Core question

> Is love present but arriving in a form the partner does not recognize?

### Common pattern

```text
Person A Expresses Love Through Action
↓
Person B Needs Emotional Acknowledgment
↓
A Feels Unappreciated
↓
B Feels Unloved
```

Or:

```text
Person A Offers Reassurance Through Words
↓
Person B Needs Practical Follow-Through
↓
A Feels Rejected
↓
B Feels Unsupported
```

### Evidence

- empathy
- recognition
- practicality
- thinking_style
- decision_style
- conflict_style

### Output requirement

The report must show both sides fairly:

- what the person intended
- what the partner received
- why the signal was lost
- what translation would work better

## 23. Strength Under Stress

Every major trait must be shown as a reversible process.

### Canonical structure

```text
Core Strength
↓
Stress Trigger
↓
Stress Flip
↓
Partner Experience
↓
Return Path
```

### Examples

| Core strength | Under stress | Partner may experience |
|---|---|---|
| steadiness | delayed response | emotional distance |
| sensitivity | hypervigilance | volatility or pressure |
| decisiveness | unilateral action | control |
| flexibility | lack of closure | unreliability |
| responsibility | overfunctioning | criticism or domination |
| independence | withdrawal | abandonment |
| closeness | repeated checking | intrusion |
| optimism | minimizing problems | invalidation |

### Product rule

No trait should be described only as a strength or only as a weakness.

---

## 24. Couple Conflict Loops

A useful Partnership report must identify loops, not villains.

### Common loop families

#### 24.1 Pursue–Withdraw

```text
One Partner Seeks Immediate Resolution
↓
The Other Needs Distance
↓
Distance Feels Like Rejection
↓
Pursuit Intensifies
↓
Pressure Feels Unsafe
↓
Withdrawal Deepens
```

#### 24.2 Control–Resistance

```text
One Partner Anticipates Risk
↓
Creates More Rules or Monitoring
↓
The Other Feels Managed
↓
Resists or Delays
↓
The First Partner Trusts Less
```

#### 24.3 Overfunction–Underfunction

```text
One Partner Takes Over
↓
The Other Loses Ownership
↓
The First Becomes Exhausted
↓
The Second Feels Criticized or Incapable
↓
The First Takes Over More
```

#### 24.4 Logic–Emotion Mismatch

```text
One Partner Seeks Facts and Solutions
↓
The Other Seeks Emotional Recognition
↓
Solutions Feel Cold
↓
Emotion Feels Irrational
↓
Both Feel Unheard
```

### Output rule

Every conflict loop must include:

- trigger
- each person’s protective intention
- each person’s interpretation
- escalation point
- interruption point
- recovery action

# PART VII. Remember This Person

## 25. Partner Kick Point

### Purpose

This is the emotional center of the Partnership product.

It answers:

> When I am angry, exhausted, or disappointed, what am I most likely to forget about why this person matters?

### Possible section names

Primary:

- **Remember This Person**

Alternates:

- Why I Still Choose You
- Your Partner’s Hidden Gift
- The Reason They Are Still Your Person
- What They Bring When Life Gets Hard

### Content requirements

A Kick Point must include:

1. **Specific contribution**
2. **Concrete scene**
3. **What becomes invisible during conflict**
4. **Why the trait still matters**
5. **No sentimental exaggeration**

### Good example

> When everything becomes uncertain, this person is often the one who keeps moving, handles the immediate problem, or refuses to abandon the situation. Their directness may frustrate you in ordinary moments, but in a crisis it can become the reason the two of you do not collapse at the same time.

### Bad example

> This person is your destiny and will always protect you.

### Evidence requirement

Kick Point cannot be generic praise.

It must be supported by at least two sources where available, such as:

- behavioral axis
- relationship interaction
- reliability pattern
- conflict pattern
- complementary role
- repeated real-life tendency

---

## 26. Couple Kick Point

In addition to each partner’s individual gift, the report may identify what the couple itself does well.

Examples:

- stabilizes each other under uncertainty
- turns ideas into action
- creates emotional warmth after hardship
- protects family continuity
- balances courage and caution
- combines realism with hope

### Product frame

> This is not proof that the relationship will last. It is a capability the relationship can deliberately use.

# PART VIII. Life Direction

## 27. Shared Life Direction

### Core question

> Are these two people trying to build the same kind of life?

### Life-direction dimensions

- stability
- growth
- freedom
- family closeness
- career ambition
- financial security
- social life
- home ownership
- mobility
- parenting
- caregiving
- lifestyle intensity
- retirement vision

### Classification model

#### Shared Direction

Same destination and broadly compatible route.

#### Different Route

Same destination, different preferred method or pace.

#### Negotiable Difference

Different preferences that can be scheduled, divided, or alternated.

#### Structural Tension

A difference involving fundamental life design.

Examples:

- whether to have children
- where to live long term
- monogamy expectations
- whether to support extended family financially
- whether one person should permanently subordinate a career

### Product rule

Structural tension must never be softened into a cute personality difference.

It should be named clearly as a life conversation requiring explicit decision.

## 28. The Life You Are Building

This chapter synthesizes the couple’s likely shared-life shape.

It may include:

- emotional climate
- operational rhythm
- financial posture
- family boundary style
- decision pattern
- growth potential
- main system vulnerability
- strongest joint capability

### Required language

Use:

- “may become”
- “is likely to feel”
- “works best when”
- “can become costly if”

Avoid:

- “will become”
- “is destined to”
- “must”
- “guarantees”

# PART IX. Repair and Long-Term Growth

## 29. Repair Architecture

### Core question

> After the rupture, how do these two people return?

### Evidence

- conflict_style
- resilience
- empathy
- self_control
- recognition
- decision_style

### Repair stages

```text
Stop Escalation
↓
Regulate
↓
Acknowledge Impact
↓
Clarify Intention
↓
Repair the Practical Damage
↓
Rebuild Trust Through Follow-Through
```

### Product distinction

Apology is not the same as repair.

Repair may require:

- emotional acknowledgment
- behavior change
- financial correction
- schedule change
- boundary enforcement
- task ownership
- repeated follow-through

---

## 30. Daily Rituals

Long-term partnership is shaped by repeated micro-signals.

Possible recommendations:

- brief daily check-in
- visible task ownership
- weekly planning
- individual rest protection
- financial review cadence
- appreciation ritual
- conflict pause agreement
- family-boundary check
- protected couple time

### Product rule

Recommendations must be proportional and realistic.

Avoid overwhelming users with a perfect-relationship program.

Prefer one or two high-leverage adjustments per module.

---

## 31. Growing Old Together

### Purpose

Close the report with maturity rather than fantasy.

Questions:

- What becomes more valuable with time?
- What pattern becomes more dangerous if left unexamined?
- What should the couple protect before crisis forces the issue?
- How can their strengths age well?

### Required frame

> Long-term partnership is not maintained by never changing. It is maintained by continuing to update the relationship as both people change.

---

## 32. Final Chapter — Why This Is Still Your Person

The final chapter reconnects love and reality.

It should not erase conflict or force gratitude.

It should say, in effect:

- You have now seen the system honestly.
- Here is what is difficult.
- Here is what remains valuable.
- Here is what requires choice.
- Here is what the two of you can do next.

### Closing principle

> A good Partnership report should leave the user neither falsely reassured nor emotionally defeated. It should leave them more able to see the person, the pattern, and the next conversation.

Visuals must clarify the relationship system, not pretend to provide objective destiny scores.

### Recommended

#### Ownership Matrix

Shows who notices, plans, initiates, completes, and follows up.

#### Paired Continua

Examples:

- structure ↔ flexibility
- caution ↔ speed
- solitude ↔ togetherness
- saving ↔ experience spending

Shows trigger, response, interpretation, escalation, and interruption.

#### Strength-to-Cost Flip

Shows how a valuable trait changes under stress.

# PART XI. Language and Editorial Rules

## 35. Tone

The voice should be:

- warm
- intelligent
- direct
- emotionally observant
- practical
- non-clinical
- non-mystical
- respectful of complexity

The tone should feel like a perceptive friend who sees both the love and the system.

---

## 36. Required Editorial Pattern

Where possible, use:

```text
Observation
↓
Translation
↓
Relational Cost
↓
Adjustment
```

Example:

> One partner tends to move quickly once a decision feels clear, while the other needs more time to inspect consequences. The first may experience questions as resistance; the second may experience speed as exclusion. The problem is not simply pace. It is whether both people still feel represented in the decision. Set a threshold: small reversible choices can be fast, while high-impact decisions require explicit consultation.

---

## 37. Forbidden Language

Never use:

- fate
- destiny
- perfect match
- doomed
- naturally superior spouse
- traditional technical fortune-telling jargon
- gendered household assumptions
- guaranteed future claims
- diagnostic labels without clinical basis
- “always” and “never” without direct user evidence

Avoid:

- vague praise
- generic “communicate more” advice
- symmetrical language when the burden is clearly asymmetric
- false balance that erases unequal labor

---

## 38. Name and Address Policy

- Prefer partner names when available.
- Avoid repetitive “Person A / Person B” in user-facing prose.
- Avoid language that sounds accusatory.
- Use role-based references in conflict examples when helpful:
  - the partner seeking immediate resolution
  - the partner needing space
  - the partner carrying the mental load
  - the partner making the final call

# PART XII. Safety and Ethical Boundaries

## 39. Absolute Prohibitions

The product must not:

- predict divorce
- predict abuse
- diagnose abuse from compatibility data
- predict illness
- predict fertility
- predict child traits or success
- assign moral worth
- advise staying in an unsafe relationship
- use mystical authority to override current behavior

---

## 40. Safety Escalation Boundary

Normal relationship optimization is inappropriate when user-provided information includes:

- fear of retaliation
- threats
- stalking
- forced financial dependency
- physical violence
- sexual coercion
- isolation
- restricted access to money, communication, or movement

In such cases, the product must not frame the problem as a mutual communication mismatch.

It should exit the normal relationship-analysis mode and guide the user toward appropriate support.

---

## 41. Growth Without Blame

The report should distinguish:

- difference
- friction
- repeated harm
- structural incompatibility
- safety concern

Not every difference is a problem.

Not every recurring problem is harmless.

Not every conflict is equally shared.

The product must remain compassionate without becoming vague or falsely symmetrical.

---

# PART XIII. Data-to-Narrative Rules

## 42. Synthesis Priority

A Partnership insight should ideally use multiple evidence sources.

Preferred:

```text
Behavior Axis
+
Partner Difference
+
Interaction Pattern
+
Shared-Life Domain
```

Weak output:

> You are practical.

Strong output:

> You are more likely to express care by solving concrete problems and keeping life moving. That becomes a major strength when the household is under pressure, but it can miss the emotional recognition your partner needs before they are ready to discuss solutions.

---

## 43. Confidence Behavior

The report must vary tone by evidence strength.

### Strong evidence

- direct and specific
- still non-deterministic

### Mixed evidence

- describe competing tendencies
- prioritize current behavior

### Limited evidence

- present as possibility
- avoid scene-level certainty

### Null evidence

- omit the claim
- do not fill with generic prose

---

## 44. Current Behavior Overrides Underlying Tendency

When current psychology conflicts with a deeper tendency signal:

> An underlying pull toward spontaneity may exist, but your current behavior shows stronger planning and self-regulation. In daily life, your partner is more likely to experience the organized version of you.

The output should respect learned behavior, maturity, culture, and deliberate change.

# PART XV. Product Success Criteria

## 47. A Successful Partnership Report Must

- feel specific to the couple
- preserve love without romanticizing dysfunction
- explain real shared-life scenes
- identify invisible labor
- show both contribution and cost
- distinguish preference from structural life conflict
- identify at least one couple loop
- provide at least one meaningful Kick Point
- end with agency
- avoid all fortune-telling terminology
- prioritize current behavior
- never reduce the relationship to a score

---

## 48. Failure Modes

The product fails if it becomes:

- a generic marriage article
- a chore-splitting worksheet only
- a softened fortune-telling report
- a Romantic report with money added
- a moral judgment about one partner
- a symmetrical report that ignores unequal burden
- a prediction engine
- a list of 11-axis labels
- an overly clinical counseling imitation
- an inspirational love letter unsupported by evidence

# PART XVI. Final Product Thesis

Partnership is the Ahaitsme product that explains:

> **How love survives real life.**

It does this by showing:

```text
Why You Chose Each Other
+
What You Build Together
+
Where the System Hurts
+
What Each Person Still Brings
+
What Must Be Chosen Deliberately
```

The product does not ask only whether the couple is compatible.

It asks:

- Can they see the system they have created?
- Can they recognize what each person carries?
- Can they distinguish love from the form in which love is delivered?
- Can they interrupt the loop before it becomes identity?
- Can they make explicit decisions about the life they are building?
- Can they remember the person beneath the problem?

> **We do not evaluate whether the relationship deserves to exist. We help the couple understand how it is living.**
