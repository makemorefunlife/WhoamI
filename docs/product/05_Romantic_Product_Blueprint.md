## 0. Document Purpose

> What must the Romantic experience deliver for users to feel that Ahaitsme understood their relationship deeply, honestly, and usefully?

# Part 1. Romantic Product Definition

## 1. Why This Product Exists

Romantic relationships create questions that are emotionally urgent but difficult to answer clearly.

Users rarely open a relationship report because they want abstract personality information. They open it because they are trying to understand something that feels uncertain, painful, exciting, or important.

They want to know:

- whether the relationship is meaningful
- whether they are truly compatible
- why attraction and frustration coexist
- why the same misunderstandings repeat
- whether the relationship can become safer and stronger
- what each person can actually do differently
- what the future of the relationship may feel like

Most compatibility products reduce these questions to one score, one type match, or a list of disconnected traits.

Ahaitsme must do something more valuable.

It must help two people see the relationship as a living system:

- what naturally pulls them together
- what creates emotional safety
- where they misread each other
- how their different rhythms interact
- what roles they unconsciously take
- how conflict escalates and recovers
- which actions would genuinely improve the relationship

The Romantic product exists to turn emotional confusion into recognizable relationship patterns and practical choices.

---

## 2. Product Promise

The Romantic report should make users feel:

> “This does not just describe each of us. It explains what happens between us.”

The product must deliver five forms of value.

| Value | Product promise |
|---|---|
| Recognition | Users recognize their real relationship, not generic personality descriptions. |
| Explanation | Users understand why attraction, conflict, distance, and reassurance happen. |
| Translation | Users understand how the same moment can feel different to each person. |
| Direction | Users know what to do differently, not only what the problem is. |
| Perspective | Users can see both the relationship’s strengths and limits without fatalism. |

The product must never promise certainty about love, marriage, breakup, or destiny.

It should provide an honest interpretation of tendencies, interaction patterns, and likely pressure points.

---

## 3. Who This Product Is For

The Romantic product is designed for people who are emotionally invested enough to want more than entertainment.

Primary users include:

### 3.1 Early relationship users

They want to understand attraction, compatibility, pace, emotional style, and future potential.

### 3.2 Established couples

They want to understand repeated misunderstandings, differences in affection, conflict patterns, roles, and long-term stability.

### 3.3 Couples under strain

They want language for what is going wrong and practical ways to interrupt recurring patterns.

### 3.4 Reflective solo viewers

They may be viewing the report alone to better understand the relationship, their partner, or their own behavior within the relationship.

### 3.5 Users considering long-term commitment

They want a more mature view of how the relationship may function over time, beyond attraction alone.

The product is not designed to diagnose abuse, determine whether someone should stay or leave, or replace professional relationship counseling.

# Part 2. Romantic User Questions Canon

## 4. The Questions Users Actually Bring

The Romantic experience must be designed around real user questions, not around available data fields.

The following questions form the Romantic User Questions Canon.

### 4.1 Immediate compatibility

- Are we actually a good match?
- What makes us feel drawn to each other?
- Is this relationship naturally easy or naturally intense?
- What is the strongest part of our connection?
- Where are we most likely to struggle?

### 4.2 Emotional safety and affection

- Do we feel loved in the same way?
- Why does one person need more reassurance?
- Why can affection feel uneven even when both people care?
- Who tends to approach, wait, pursue, or withdraw?
- What makes each person feel emotionally secure?

### 4.3 Misunderstanding and conflict

- Why do we keep having the same fight?
- Why does one person react faster while the other takes longer?
- Why does one person recover while the other still feels hurt?
- What does each person think is happening during conflict?
- What are we trying to communicate but failing to express?

### 4.4 Difference and complementarity

- Are we similar or complementary?
- Which differences help us?
- Which differences create friction?
- Where do we naturally understand each other?
- Where do we need deliberate translation?

### 4.5 Roles and relationship balance

- Who usually initiates?
- Who sets the emotional tone?
- Who tends to decide, organize, stabilize, or adapt?
- Are our roles balanced or becoming rigid?
- What role does each person unconsciously fall into?

### 4.6 Durability and future

- Can this relationship last?
- What would make it stronger over time?
- What pressure could weaken it?
- How might the relationship change through different periods?
- What should we pay attention to before making a deeper commitment?

### 4.7 Action

- What should I do differently?
- What should my partner understand about me?
- What should we stop doing?
- What conversation do we need to have?
- What small change would make the biggest difference?

---

## 5. Priority User Jobs

The product must satisfy four primary jobs before asking users to read deeper interpretation.

| Priority | User job | Required answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | “우리 잘 맞아?” | A clear relationship signature with situational nuance. |
| 2 | “왜 자꾸 싸우거나 오해해?” | The mechanism behind recurring misreads and conflict. |
| 3 | “그래서 뭘 해야 해?” | Specific, person-shaped actions and communication guidance. |
| 4 | “앞으로 어떻게 될까?” | A careful, non-fatalistic view of relationship development over time. |

The first mobile experience must answer the first three jobs quickly enough that the user feels immediate value.

The fourth job belongs primarily to the deeper reading experience.

# Part 3. Romantic Experience Journey

## 6. Emotional Journey

The report should move the user through the following emotional sequence.

```text
Curiosity
  ↓
Recognition
  ↓
Relief
  ↓
Deeper understanding
  ↓
Mutual translation
  ↓
Practical direction
  ↓
Hopeful realism
```

### 6.1 Curiosity

The opening must feel specific, emotionally relevant, and worth continuing.

### 6.2 Recognition

Users should quickly recognize the relationship’s central pattern.

### 6.3 Relief

The product should help users see that many conflicts are interaction patterns rather than proof that one person does not care.

### 6.4 Deeper understanding

Users should understand how attraction, emotional style, differences, and conflict mechanisms connect.

### 6.5 Mutual translation

The product should make both perspectives understandable without turning one person into the problem.

### 6.6 Practical direction

The report should move from insight to concrete action.

### 6.7 Hopeful realism

The conclusion should neither romanticize the relationship nor reduce it to risk. It should clarify what can grow, what requires effort, and what may remain difficult.

---

## 7. Answer Order

The Romantic report should answer questions in the order users are emotionally ready to receive them.

```text
1. What kind of relationship is this?
2. Where are we naturally strong?
3. Where do we experience the relationship differently?
4. Why do we misread each other?
5. What roles do we fall into?
6. What happens during conflict?
7. What can we do differently?
8. What might this relationship become over time?
```

This order protects the experience from becoming either overly flattering or prematurely negative.

Recognition must come before advice.

Strength must appear before risk.

Difference must be explained before it is treated as a problem.

# Part 4. Romantic Report Architecture

## 8. Complete Product Structure

The Romantic report uses the shared relationship experience system defined in `04_Relationship_Experience_Blueprint.md`, expressed through the following Romantic-specific sequence.

```text
A. Relationship Hero
B. Relationship at a Glance
C. Why We Misread Each Other
D. Our Difference Map
E. Relationship Roles
F. Conflict Translation
G. Relationship Guide
H. Deep Read
I. Timeline and Future Perspective
J. Save, Share, and Revisit
```

Each section must have a unique job.

No section should repeat the same insight using different wording.

---

## 9. Section A. Relationship Hero

### Purpose

Give the user an immediate and memorable understanding of the relationship.

### Primary user question

> “What kind of relationship are we?”

### Required experience

The Hero should include:

- a relationship signature
- a concise one-line interpretation
- a strong emotional visual identity
- three primary relationship dimensions
- a clear invitation to continue

### Three primary dimensions

The opening experience should organize the relationship into three user-comprehensible questions:

| Dimension | User meaning |
|---|---|
| Connection | How naturally do closeness, affection, and attraction form? |
| Stability | How well can the relationship sustain trust, rhythm, and continuity? |
| Sensitivity | How easily do differences become tension, misunderstanding, or emotional reactivity? |

These dimensions should not be presented as a final verdict.

They are an orientation system for the rest of the report.

### Success criteria

Within seconds, the user should be able to say:

> “I understand the overall character of our relationship, but I also want to know why.”

---

## 10. Section B. Relationship at a Glance

### Purpose

Show how the relationship changes across meaningful situations.

### Primary user question

> “Where do we work well, and where does it become difficult?”

### Core situations

The product should prioritize situations that are genuinely supported and emotionally meaningful.

| Situation | Product question |
|---|---|
| When we are close | What makes intimacy and connection work? |
| When daily life continues | What supports or weakens stability? |
| When tension appears | What makes conflict or sensitivity increase? |

Unsupported lifestyle situations must not be presented as scored truths merely because they would be visually attractive.

### Success criteria

The user should understand that compatibility is contextual rather than one-dimensional.

---

## 11. Section C. Why We Misread Each Other

### Purpose

Explain the most important emotional translation gaps in the relationship.

### Primary user question

> “Why can we both care and still feel misunderstood?”

### Core themes

- reassurance needs
- affection signals
- expression timing
- conflict response timing
- emotional recovery
- lingering emotional residue

These themes must be connected into recognizable interaction patterns.

They should not appear as unrelated labels.

### Required product effect

Users should be able to recognize moments such as:

- one person asks for reassurance while the other assumes their care is already obvious
- one person speaks immediately while the other needs time to organize feelings
- one person believes the conflict is over while the other is still emotionally processing it
- one person’s practical response feels emotionally distant to the other

### Success criteria

The section succeeds when the user feels:

> “This is why we keep missing each other.”

---

## 12. Section D. Our Difference Map

### Purpose

Help users distinguish healthy difference, useful complementarity, and recurring friction.

### Primary user question

> “How are we similar, and where are we fundamentally different?”

### Core experience

The Difference Map should organize information into three categories:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Shared ground | Areas where the pair naturally understands or supports each other. |
| Complementary difference | Areas where different strengths can work together. |
| Translation required | Areas where difference is likely to produce repeated misunderstanding. |

### Psychological comparison

The report may use visual comparison to show meaningful differences in areas such as:

- emotional empathy
- conflict style
- structure
- practicality
- self-control
- recognition needs
- energy style
- thinking style
- decision style
- resilience
- stimulation needs

The visual system must distinguish between:

- similarity
- difference
- compatibility
- quality

A large difference is not automatically bad.

A high score is not automatically better.

### Success criteria

The user should understand not only where the two people differ, but what that difference does inside the relationship.

---

## 13. Section E. Relationship Roles

### Purpose

Reveal how the pair organizes initiative, emotional labor, decisions, and stability.

### Primary user question

> “What role does each of us tend to take?”

### Core themes

- who initiates connection
- who sets emotional pace
- who introduces ideas or change
- who stabilizes or organizes
- who decides
- who adapts
- who pursues
- who withdraws
- which role each person unconsciously performs

### Product rule

Roles are patterns, not permanent identities.

The report must not label one person as the leader, parent, dependent, avoidant, or problem without sufficient context.

The product should show when roles are:

- naturally complementary
- uneven but workable
- rigid and exhausting
- frequently reversed by situation

### Success criteria

The user should recognize both the usefulness and cost of the roles they currently occupy.

---

## 14. Section F. Conflict Translation

### Purpose

Turn conflict analysis into understandable moments and speakable alternatives.

### Primary user question

> “What happens between us when things go wrong?”

### Required structure

Each conflict pattern should make four things clear:

1. the situation that activates the pattern
2. what each person tends to do
3. why the responses collide
4. what a better response could sound like

### Experience pattern

```text
Trigger
  ↓
Person A response
  ↓
Person B interpretation or response
  ↓
Escalation loop
  ↓
Translation
  ↓
Better line or action
```

### Dialogue principle

The product should provide realistic, speakable language.

It must not create theatrical scripts, therapeutic jargon, or unnaturally perfect dialogue.

### Success criteria

The user should leave with at least one sentence they could genuinely use in the next difficult conversation.

---

## 15. Section G. Relationship Guide

### Purpose

Convert understanding into practical relationship behavior.

### Primary user question

> “What should we actually do?”

### Action categories

The guide should prioritize a limited number of high-value actions.

| Category | Example product job |
|---|---|
| Continue | Protect a strength already helping the relationship. |
| Translate | Explain one recurring difference more clearly. |
| Interrupt | Stop one escalation loop. |
| Practice | Build one new relational habit. |
| Discuss | Have one necessary conversation. |
| Watch | Notice one pressure point before it becomes structural. |

### Action quality rules

Every action should be:

- connected to a relationship pattern already explained
- specific enough to try
- respectful to both people
- realistic for ordinary life
- free of generic relationship clichés

Advice should never appear before users understand why it applies.

### Success criteria

The user should know the next useful step, not merely feel informed.

---

## 16. Section H. Deep Read

### Purpose

Reward users who want a richer understanding of each person and the bond.

### Primary user question

> “What is happening beneath the visible relationship?”

### Deep Read modules

- individual relationship nature
- hidden emotional needs
- what each person brings to the other
- what makes the bond feel special
- deeper attraction and tension patterns
- internal feelings that may not be openly expressed
- long-form synthesis of the relationship

### Product rule

Deep Read must deepen the main report, not restart it.

It should not repeat earlier cards in paragraph form.

It should reveal connections that become meaningful only after the user understands the core relationship structure.

### Success criteria

The user should feel that the deeper layer adds meaning rather than volume.

---

## 17. Section I. Timeline and Future Perspective

### Purpose

Provide a careful view of how the relationship may feel across time and changing circumstances.

### Primary user question

> “How might this relationship develop?”

### Product role

The timeline is a distinctive Romantic value because it helps users think about relationship development rather than only static compatibility.

It may explore:

- periods of easier connection
- periods of pressure or sensitivity
- shifts in priorities
- windows for commitment, repair, or change
- themes that may become more important over time

### Future language rule

The product must use tendency language, not certainty language.

It should never claim that a breakup, marriage, betrayal, or specific event will happen.

### Success criteria

The user should gain perspective without feeling trapped by prediction.

---

## 18. Section J. Save, Share, and Revisit

### Purpose

Allow the report to become a relationship object rather than a one-time reading.

### Potential experiences

- a shareable relationship signature
- saved insight cards
- selected conversation prompts
- partner-friendly summary
- revisitable action plan
- future comparison after retaking the psychological survey

### Product rule

Sharing should preserve dignity and nuance.

The product should never encourage users to weaponize a score, label, or insight against their partner.

---

# Part 5. Visual Meaning System

## 19. Role of Cards

Cards should provide fast recognition and entry points into deeper explanation.

Good card subjects include:

- relationship signature
- connection, stability, and sensitivity
- reassurance pattern
- expression timing
- recovery pattern
- relationship roles
- strengths and friction
- key actions

Cards should not fragment one relationship pattern into many disconnected facts.

---

## 20. Role of Graphs

Graphs should clarify meaningful comparison, proportion, or relationship structure.

Appropriate uses include:

- situational relationship dimensions
- psychological comparison
- balance between two people
- relationship chemistry dimensions
- role distribution where a meaningful scale exists

Graphs must not turn categories, uncertain judgments, or narrative meanings into fake precision.

A graph must always answer a user question more clearly than prose alone.

---

## 21. Role of Comparison Tables

Comparison tables should help users translate differences between two people.

Each row should explain:

- how Person A tends to approach the area
- how Person B tends to approach the area
- what happens between them
- whether the difference creates ease, complementarity, or friction

The table should not become a dense inventory of traits.

It must remain readable on mobile and understandable without technical vocabulary.

---

## 22. Role of Dialogue

Dialogue is the bridge from insight to action.

It should show:

- the familiar response
- why it fails
- a more effective alternative

Dialogue must sound like real people speaking.

It should remain short, specific, and emotionally believable.

---

## 23. Role of Timeline

The timeline should provide orientation, not fate.

Its visual language should communicate:

- movement
- changing emphasis
- opportunity
- pressure
- uncertainty

It should avoid countdowns, fixed outcomes, or alarmist risk markers.

---

# Part 6. Premium Product Value

## 24. Free Experience

The free Romantic experience should provide enough value to establish trust.

It should answer:

- the overall relationship signature
- the primary strengths
- one important difference
- one meaningful interaction insight
- a preview of what deeper analysis can reveal

The free experience must feel complete enough to be useful, not intentionally broken.

---

## 25. Premium Experience

Premium should not mean simply more text.

Premium value should come from deeper synthesis, translation, and action.

The premium report should provide:

- full relationship architecture
- detailed difference map
- reassurance and affection interpretation
- role dynamics
- conflict loops and dialogue translation
- personalized actions
- deeper individual and bond interpretation
- future perspective and timeline
- saved or shareable relationship insights

### Premium promise

> Free tells users what the relationship feels like. Premium explains why it feels that way and what to do with that understanding.

---

## 26. What Must Feel Premium

The following qualities create premium value:

- specificity
- emotional accuracy
- synthesis across multiple dimensions
- balanced treatment of both people
- useful visual explanation
- realistic communication language
- actionable guidance
- elegant pacing
- absence of generic filler

Length alone is not premium.

Complexity alone is not premium.

Technical vocabulary is not premium.

# Part 7. Romantic Product Principles

## 27. Romantic-Specific Design Principles

### 27.1 Relationship over individuals

The product must explain what happens between two people, not merely place two personality reports side by side.

### 27.2 Attraction is not compatibility

Strong chemistry may coexist with instability, emotional misreading, or difficult conflict.

The product must preserve this distinction.

### 27.3 Difference is not defect

A mismatch should first be understood as a translation problem before it is treated as a weakness.

### 27.4 Recognition before advice

Users must feel seen before they are told what to do.

### 27.5 Both people retain dignity

No one should become the villain, the emotionally inferior partner, or the fixed problem.

### 27.6 Insight must lead somewhere

Every major friction pattern should eventually connect to a useful action, conversation, or expectation.

### 27.7 No fake certainty

The report must never turn tendencies into destiny.

### 27.8 No isolated fortune storytelling

Traditional signals may inform interpretation, but user-facing meaning must describe the relationship pattern in ordinary language.

### 27.9 Emotional honesty over reassurance

The product should not soften every difficult finding into a positive statement.

It should communicate risk carefully, respectfully, and clearly.

### 27.10 One relationship, one coherent story

The report should feel like one connected interpretation rather than a collection of cards produced by separate systems.

# Part 8. Product Boundaries

## 28. What This Product Must Not Claim

The Romantic product must not claim to determine:

- whether two people should stay together
- whether someone is a good or bad partner
- whether marriage will definitely happen
- whether a breakup will definitely happen
- whether cheating will occur
- whether one person loves more
- whether one person is psychologically disordered
- whether the relationship is abusive

Where a product insight touches serious emotional harm or safety, the report should clearly stay within its interpretive limits.

---

## 29. Unsupported Product Areas

The product should not create scored sections merely because users may be curious about them.

Areas such as the following require sufficient product evidence before becoming formal report modules:

- physical or sexual chemistry as a deterministic score
- money management compatibility
- household labor and chores
- social behavior around other people
- personal space needs
- parenting compatibility
- fidelity risk

These may become future research or product expansion areas, but should not be presented as authoritative without proper foundations.

---

# Part 9. What Users Should Remember

## 30. Memory Outcomes

A world-class Romantic report should leave users remembering no more than a few powerful truths.

The ideal memory structure is:

1. **Our relationship signature**  
   What kind of relationship this is.

2. **Our strongest connection**  
   What naturally brings us together.

3. **Our central misunderstanding**  
   Where we repeatedly misread each other.

4. **Our relationship roles**  
   What each person tends to carry or perform.

5. **Our next useful step**  
   What would make the relationship work better.

6. **Our future watchpoint**  
   What deserves attention as the relationship develops.

If users remember twenty labels but cannot explain these six things, the product has failed.

# Part 10. World-Class Standard

## 31. What Makes the Romantic Report Feel World-Class

The report should feel world-class when:

- the first screen is emotionally compelling and immediately understandable
- the relationship feels specifically recognized
- visual elements make complexity easier, not more decorative
- each section answers a real user question
- both people feel accurately and respectfully represented
- difficult findings are honest without becoming deterministic
- long-form writing connects multiple patterns into one coherent relationship story
- users receive language they can actually use with each other
- advice feels earned by the preceding interpretation
- the report remains elegant and clear on mobile
- premium value is obvious without artificial withholding
- users want to save, share, or revisit the report

The standard is not whether the report contains more information than competitors.

The standard is whether it creates a clearer, deeper, and more useful understanding of the relationship.

# Part 11. Product Success Criteria

## 32. User Success Criteria

The Romantic report succeeds when users can answer:

- What kind of relationship do we have?
- What brings us together?
- Where do we misunderstand each other?
- What roles do we tend to take?
- What happens when we fight?
- What can we do differently?
- What should we pay attention to over time?

---

## 33. Experience Success Criteria

- The central relationship pattern is recognizable within the first mobile scroll.
- The first three sections provide value without requiring technical knowledge.
- Every major visual answers a specific user question.
- Every major friction pattern includes explanation before advice.
- Similarity, compatibility, and quality are never treated as the same concept.
- Both people retain dignity throughout the experience.
- The Deep Read adds new meaning rather than repetition.
- Future interpretation uses uncertainty responsibly.
- Unsupported topics are not presented as authoritative scores.
- The report feels like one coherent product rather than a collection of generated modules.

---

## 34. Business Success Criteria

- Free users understand the product’s unique value.
- Premium value is visible through depth, synthesis, and action rather than word count.
- The report creates natural save, share, and revisit behavior.
- The Romantic structure becomes a useful reference for later Friend, Work, and Family product blueprints without forcing those products into Romantic logic.
- Product meaning remains stable even if implementation changes.

# Final Product Statement

The Ahaitsme Romantic product is not a compatibility score and not a fortune-reading summary.

It is a relationship understanding experience.

Its purpose is to help two people recognize what connects them, understand what repeatedly separates them, translate their differences, and make wiser choices about how they love each other.

The product succeeds when users do not merely say:

> “This is accurate.”

They should also be able to say:

> “Now I understand what happens between us, and I know what we can do next.”
