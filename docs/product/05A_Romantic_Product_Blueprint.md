## 0. Product Manifesto

People do not come to Ahaitsme because they want their relationship graded.

They come because they want to understand:

- why the same misunderstanding keeps repeating,
- why one person reaches out while the other goes quiet,
- why they feel unusually safe, excited, exhausted, drawn in, or confused,
- why this relationship feels different from other relationships,
- and what they can actually do next.

Ahaitsme Romantic does not classify relationships as good or bad.

It does not tell people whether they should stay or leave.

It does not reduce two people to a score, a rank, a grade, a symbol, or a single compatibility verdict.

Instead, it helps users recognize the relationship they are already living.

The user should move through the experience thinking:

> “어? 맞다.”  
> “우리 진짜 이러는데.”  
> “그래서 그랬던 거였구나.”  
> “상대는 그렇게 느끼고 있었구나.”  
> “다음에는 이렇게 해봐야겠다.”

We are not building a relationship report.

We are building a **relationship experience**.

The user should not feel like they are reading an analysis sheet. They should feel like they are watching their own relationship unfold as a story—one that is accurate enough to recognize, layered enough to keep reading, kind enough to feel safe, and practical enough to change what happens next.

When the experience ends, the user should remember not a score, but a sentence, a realization, a scene, or a new way to respond.

# 1. Product Definition

## 1.1 What this product is

Ahaitsme Romantic is a guided storytelling experience that translates relationship evidence into a clear emotional narrative.

It answers five enduring human questions:

1. **How are we different?**
2. **How does this relationship tend to move?**
3. **Why does this relationship feel special?**
4. **What should we do—and stop doing—with each other?**
5. **What can we actually try next?**

These five questions are the stable content foundation of the product.

They are not displayed as five rigid numbered chapters. They are woven into a narrative journey that moves from recognition to understanding to action.

---

## 1.2 What this product is not

Ahaitsme Romantic is not:

- a compatibility score,
- a relationship test result sheet,
- a verdict about whether the relationship is “good,”
- a deterministic prediction,
- a list of personality traits,
- a horoscope-style metaphor generator,
- a generic advice article,
- a marriage operations report,
- a diagnostic or clinical assessment,
- or a collection of disconnected cards.

The product may use scores, axes, signals, and structured evidence internally. Those elements must never become the user’s main experience.

---

## 1.3 Product promise

The product promise is:

> **“We help you see the relationship you are already living—more clearly, more kindly, and more usefully.”**

Users should feel:

> “This does not just describe each of us. It explains what happens between us.”

The product must deliver these outcomes:

### Recognition
The user sees familiar scenes, patterns, and differences.

### Reframing
The user understands why those patterns happen without reducing either person to blame—including mutual translation of the same moment.

### Response
The user leaves with a more effective way to act, speak, pause, repair, or reconnect.

### Perspective
The user can see both the relationship’s strengths and limits without fatalism, destiny claims, or a stay/leave verdict.

The product must never promise certainty about love, marriage, breakup, or destiny. It interprets tendencies, interaction patterns, and likely pressure points.

### Attraction is not compatibility

Strong chemistry may coexist with instability, emotional misreading, or difficult conflict. The product must preserve this distinction.

---

## 1.4 Who this product is for

The Romantic product is for people emotionally invested enough to want more than entertainment.

| Audience | What they typically need |
|---|---|
| **Early relationship** | Attraction, pace, emotional style, future potential |
| **Established couples** | Repeated misunderstandings, affection differences, conflict, roles, long-term emotional stability |
| **Couples under strain** | Language for what is going wrong and ways to interrupt recurring patterns |
| **Reflective solo viewers** | Understanding the relationship, the partner, or their own behavior within it |
| **Users considering deeper commitment** | A mature view of how the bond may function over time—beyond attraction alone |

The product is not designed to diagnose abuse, decide whether someone should stay or leave, or replace professional counseling.

# 2. Product Boundaries

## 2.1 Romantic owns

Romantic owns the emotional and relational experience of being a couple:

- attraction,
- affection,
- emotional rhythm,
- closeness and distance,
- reassurance,
- misunderstanding,
- expression speed,
- conflict pattern,
- repair,
- unconscious roles,
- hidden needs,
- relationship-specific growth,
- why the connection feels different,
- and how the bond may evolve emotionally.

---

## 2.2 Marriage owns

Marriage owns long-term shared operations:

- finances,
- debt,
- wealth management,
- parenting,
- household labor,
- caregiving logistics,
- long-term role division,
- housing decisions,
- legal or formal commitment logistics,
- and shared life administration.

Romantic may mention these topics only when they directly illuminate emotional dynamics. It must not become the operational decision product.

---

## 2.3 Hard boundary rule

When a topic asks:

> “How do these two people emotionally experience each other?”

it belongs to Romantic.

When a topic asks:

> “How should these two people run a shared life system?”

it belongs to Marriage.

---

## 2.4 What this product must not claim

The Romantic product must not claim to determine:

- whether two people should stay together,
- whether someone is a good or bad partner,
- whether marriage or breakup will definitely happen,
- whether cheating will occur,
- whether one person loves more,
- whether one person is psychologically disordered,
- or whether the relationship is abusive.

Where an insight touches serious emotional harm or safety, the report must stay within interpretive limits and point outside ordinary scoring when needed.

---

## 2.5 Unsupported product areas

Do not create scored or authoritative modules merely because users may be curious.

These require sufficient product evidence before becoming formal report modules:

- physical or sexual chemistry as a deterministic score,
- money management compatibility,
- household labor and chores,
- social behavior around other people as a scored lifestyle verdict,
- personal space needs as a Marriage-style operations score,
- parenting compatibility,
- fidelity risk.

They may become future research or expansion areas, but must not be presented as authoritative without foundations. Marriage-owned topics remain Marriage (see §2.2).

# 3. Product Laws

These laws are permanent. Any future design, copy, model output, component, or feature that violates them is out of scope.

## Law 1 — Story comes before analysis

The user first encounters meaning, not machinery.

Do not begin with charts, raw axes, tables, labels, or technical categories. Begin with a human truth, scene, tension, or relationship sentence.

---

## Law 2 — Evidence supports; it does not lead

The story owns the experience.

Evidence exists to make the story trustworthy, specific, and explainable.

The user should be able to inspect the evidence, but should never be forced to decode it before understanding the relationship.

---

## Law 3 — Every section answers one human question

Sections are organized around what the user is wondering—not around the backend data structure.

A section must answer one clear question such as:

- “Why do we keep missing each other?”
- “Why does one of us chase while the other shuts down?”
- “What does this person need to feel safe?”
- “Why does this relationship feel unusually intense?”
- “What can we do differently next time?”

---

## Law 4 — Recognition comes before advice

Advice without recognition feels generic.

The correct order is:

1. show the familiar pattern,
2. explain what each person may be experiencing,
3. reframe the misunderstanding,
4. then offer a practical response.

---

## Law 5 — Never judge, grade, rank, or crown

The Romantic product must not use:

- letter grades,
- star ratings,
- compatibility ranks,
- “best/worst” labels,
- winner/loser framing,
- soulmate claims,
- red/green relationship verdicts,
- or any equivalent device that collapses complexity into evaluation.

A relationship can be intense, gentle, demanding, complementary, fast-moving, slow-building, or repair-heavy without being declared good or bad.

---

## Law 6 — Difference is not defect

Every difference must be framed as:

- a contrast,
- a translation problem,
- a timing mismatch,
- a need mismatch,
- or a potential complement.

Never frame a difference as one person being more emotionally correct, mature, loving, stable, or evolved unless the evidence explicitly concerns harmful behavior—and even then, avoid moralizing.

---

## Law 7 — The relationship is the protagonist

Neither individual is the hero or villain.

The main character is the dynamic created between them.

The user should see:

- what A brings,
- what B brings,
- what happens only when they meet,
- and how that shared pattern changes under different conditions.

---

## Law 8 — Every major section contains a fresh realization

Each major module must create at least one moment of recognition, surprise, relief, or reframing.

This is the product’s **surprise density** requirement.

A section that only repeats earlier conclusions is not a section; it is duplication.

---

## Law 9 — Every difficult insight must include a path forward

The product may name strain, mismatch, avoidance, pressure, emotional overload, or recurring conflict.

It must not leave the user there.

Every difficult section needs at least one of the following:

- a reframe,
- a repair move,
- a sentence to try,
- a boundary,
- a timing adjustment,
- or a hopeful interpretation.

Hope must be credible, not artificially positive.

---

## Law 10 — The user should forget they are reading a report

The structure should feel like a story unfolding, not a database being rendered.

A correct implementation may contain cards, charts, tables, or modules, but the transitions must create continuity.

The experience should have momentum.

---

## Law 11 — No unsupported certainty

Every strong claim must be grounded in evidence.

The language must reflect evidence quality:

- **Corroborated:** direct, confident wording,
- **Refined:** specific but qualified wording,
- **Tentative:** possibility language,
- **Insufficient:** omit or explicitly acknowledge uncertainty.

The product must never invent a dramatic story simply because it sounds compelling.

---

## Law 12 — No decorative mysticism

Do not convert day stems or pillars into shallow objects such as “candle,” “mountain,” “ocean,” or similar decorative metaphors as a substitute for interpretation.

Saju concepts may inform a concise identity line only when translated into behavior, relational role, emotional tendency, or situational response.

The purpose is not to decorate the report with symbolism. The purpose is to create a meaningful sentence.

# 4. The Core Experience Model

## 4.1 The five content territories

The product is built from five stable territories.

### Territory A — How we are different

User question:

> “Why do we feel so different even when we care about each other?”

Core material:

- 11-axis relationship view,
- strongest similarities,
- strongest gaps,
- difference map,
- thinking and expression contrasts,
- social-energy contrast,
- conflict-style contrast,
- reassurance and recognition needs,
- timing mismatch,
- and common misinterpretations.

Emotional outcome:

> “We are not failing. We are translating each other poorly.”

---

### Territory B — How the relationship moves

User question:

> “What pattern does our relationship keep falling into?”

Core material:

- balance of power,
- initiative and withdrawal,
- expression speed,
- reassurance cycle,
- recovery speed,
- residual emotion,
- unconscious role play,
- closeness–distance rhythm,
- and repeating relational loops.

Emotional outcome:

> “This is the pattern—not the whole person.”

---

### Territory C — Why this relationship feels special

User question:

> “What exists between us that would not exist in the same way with someone else?”

Core material:

- A→B gift,
- B→A gift,
- hidden heart,
- unique activation,
- relationship-only behavior,
- mutual transformation,
- emotional magnetism,
- and the relationship’s defining paradox.

Emotional outcome:

> “This is why this relationship matters to me.”

---

### Territory D — What to do and what to stop doing

User question:

> “What makes things worse, and what helps?”

Core material:

- do / do not,
- before / after dialogue,
- conflict translation,
- escalation triggers,
- reassurance mistakes,
- timing rules,
- repair sequence,
- and person-specific guidance.

Emotional outcome:

> “I can see exactly where we lose each other.”

---

### Territory E — What to try next

User question:

> “What can we actually do tomorrow?”

Core material:

- one immediate action,
- one sentence to try,
- one habit,
- one repair ritual,
- one boundary or pause rule,
- one reflection prompt,
- and one future-facing relationship possibility.

Emotional outcome:

> “This is usable.”

---

## 4.2 The narrative order

The five territories are not shown as a rigid 1–5 list.

The recommended emotional sequence is:

1. **Recognition** — “This sounds like us.”
2. **Contrast** — “This is where we are different.”
3. **Pattern** — “This is what keeps happening between us.”
4. **Depth** — “This is what each person is really trying to protect or receive.”
5. **Specialness** — “This is why the relationship matters.”
6. **Repair** — “This is how we can interrupt the pattern.”
7. **Action** — “This is what to try next.”
8. **Possibility** — “This is what the relationship could become.”

This is the base journey. Individual implementation may compress or expand it, but may not reverse the core emotional logic.

**Answer-order protections** (emotional readiness):

1. What kind of relationship is this?
2. Where are we naturally strong?
3. Where do we experience the relationship differently?
4. Why do we misread each other?
5. What roles and loops do we fall into?
6. What happens during conflict?
7. What can we do differently?
8. What might this relationship become over time?

Recognition must come before advice. Strength must appear before risk. Difference must be explained before it is treated as a problem.

# 4A. User Questions Canon & Priority Jobs

The Romantic experience is designed around real user questions—not around available data fields.

## 4A.1 Questions users actually bring

### Immediate compatibility
- Are we actually a good match?
- What makes us feel drawn to each other?
- Is this relationship naturally easy or naturally intense?
- What is the strongest part of our connection?
- Where are we most likely to struggle?

### Emotional safety and affection
- Do we feel loved in the same way?
- Why does one person need more reassurance?
- Why can affection feel uneven even when both people care?
- Who tends to approach, wait, pursue, or withdraw?
- What makes each person feel emotionally secure?

### Misunderstanding and conflict
- Why do we keep having the same fight?
- Why does one person react faster while the other takes longer?
- Why does one person recover while the other still feels hurt?
- What does each person think is happening during conflict?
- What are we trying to communicate but failing to express?

### Difference and complementarity
- Are we similar or complementary?
- Which differences help us?
- Which differences create friction?
- Where do we naturally understand each other?
- Where do we need deliberate translation?

### Roles and relationship balance
- Who usually initiates?
- Who sets the emotional tone?
- Who tends to decide, organize, stabilize, or adapt?
- Are our roles balanced or becoming rigid?
- What role does each person unconsciously fall into?

### Durability and future
- Can this relationship last?
- What would make it stronger over time?
- What pressure could weaken it?
- How might the relationship change through different periods?
- What should we pay attention to before making a deeper commitment?

### Action
- What should I do differently?
- What should my partner understand about me?
- What should we stop doing?
- What conversation do we need to have?
- What small change would make the biggest difference?

These questions map into the five content territories (§4.1) and the ten modules (§7). They are not a second chapter system.

## 4A.2 Priority user jobs

The product must satisfy four primary jobs before asking users to read deeper interpretation.

| Priority | User job | Required answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | “우리 잘 맞아?” | A clear relationship signature with situational nuance—not a grade. |
| 2 | “왜 자꾸 싸우거나 오해해?” | The mechanism behind recurring misreads and conflict. |
| 3 | “그래서 뭘 해야 해?” | Specific, person-shaped actions and communication guidance. |
| 4 | “앞으로 어떻게 될까?” | A careful, non-fatalistic view of relationship development over time. |

The first mobile experience must answer jobs 1–3 quickly enough that the user feels immediate value. Job 4 belongs primarily to deeper reading (Horizon / Timeline).

# 5. Emotional Journey

## 5.1 Intended emotional curve

The experience should move through:

**Curiosity → Recognition → Surprise → Understanding → Tenderness → Relief → Agency → Hope**

Each stage has a product job.

### Curiosity
Make the user want to know what comes next.

### Recognition
Show a scene, contrast, or pattern the user immediately recognizes.

### Surprise
Reveal something non-obvious: a hidden need, a reversal, a paradox, or the other person’s likely inner logic.

### Understanding
Explain how the pattern forms without blaming either person.

### Tenderness
Help the user see the other person more generously.

### Relief
Normalize the pattern and reduce unnecessary shame.

### Agency
Offer specific moves that can change the interaction.

### Hope
End with a believable picture of growth.

---

## 5.2 “Netflix” engagement principle

The user should continue because each module creates a question that the next module answers.

Examples:

- “You express care differently.” → “So what happens when you fight?”
- “One person needs quick reassurance.” → “What does the other person do under pressure?”
- “You recover at different speeds.” → “What is each person actually afraid of?”
- “This relationship creates unusual intensity.” → “What can protect the connection?”

Transitions must create forward pull.

They must not feel like generic section headings.

---

## 5.3 Re-read value

The experience must contain parts worth revisiting during real relationship moments.

High re-read assets include:

- dialogue translations,
- conflict loop,
- repair sequence,
- do / do not guidance,
- one-line relationship definition,
- hidden heart,
- and action cards.

A successful Romantic report should function both as a first-time story and as a practical reference later.

---

# 6. Hero Experience

## 6.1 Purpose

The Hero replaces the old grade-led identity.

It must establish the emotional premise of the relationship without evaluating it.

The Hero should make the user think:

> “That is us.”

not:

> “Is this score good?”

---

## 6.2 Hero content

The Hero should contain:

1. **The two names**
2. **A relationship signature**
3. **A one-sentence relational truth**
4. **A short tension or paradox**
5. **An invitation into the journey**

Optional:

- a small contextual label,
- a relationship theme,
- a concise day-stem-informed identity line,
- one visual relationship motif,
- or a light **orientation triad** (not scores-as-verdict):

| Orientation | User meaning |
|---|---|
| **Connection** | How naturally closeness, affection, and attraction form |
| **Stability** | How well the bond can sustain trust, rhythm, and continuity |
| **Sensitivity** | How easily differences become tension, misunderstanding, or reactivity |

If used, these dimensions orient the rest of the report. They must never become letter grades, ranks, or a final compatibility verdict.

Success feel:

> “I understand the overall character of our relationship, but I also want to know why.”

---

## 6.3 Relationship signature

The signature is not a grade and not a poetic metaphor.

It is a concise interpretation of what these two people create together.

Examples of acceptable forms:

- “가까워지는 속도는 다르지만, 마음이 풀리는 지점은 비슷한 관계”
- “한 사람은 먼저 말하고, 한 사람은 늦게 정리하지만 결국 같은 곳을 향하는 관계”
- “편안함과 긴장감이 동시에 살아 있어 서로를 자꾸 성장시키는 관계”

Unacceptable forms:

- “촛불과 산의 만남”
- “운명적 불꽃 커플”
- “A+ 완벽 궁합”
- “위험한 관계”

---

## 6.4 Day-stem-informed identity line

Day stem or related saju concepts may be used to create a situation-aware one-line identity.

Rules:

- translate the concept into human behavior,
- keep it relational,
- avoid fixed personality labels,
- avoid decorative elemental imagery,
- avoid deterministic destiny language,
- connect the line to the actual pair dynamic.

Correct direction:

> “A는 감정을 먼저 밝혀 관계의 온도를 확인하고, B는 충분히 생각한 뒤 안정적으로 응답하는 쪽에 가깝습니다.”

Incorrect direction:

> “정화는 촛불이고 무토는 산이므로 두 사람은 촛불과 산입니다.”

# 7. Core Experience Architecture

The final experience is organized as modules, not numbered report parts.

Recommended architecture:

## Module 1 — Opening Scene: “This is your relationship”

Purpose:

- immediate recognition,
- establish the relationship signature,
- name the central paradox,
- create curiosity.

Primary assets:

- Hero,
- one-line relationship definition,
- short relational scene,
- optional signature chips.

Success test:

The user can repeat the relationship sentence to someone else.

---

## Module 2 — Difference Map: “This is where you meet and miss”

Purpose:

- show the most meaningful similarities and differences,
- explain what the differences feel like in daily life,
- prevent difference from being interpreted as lack of care,
- show that compatibility is contextual rather than one-dimensional.

Organize meaning into:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Shared ground** | Areas where the pair naturally understands or supports each other |
| **Complementary difference** | Different strengths that can work together |
| **Translation required** | Differences likely to produce repeated misunderstanding |

Primary assets:

- 11-axis radar,
- comparison table,
- top similarities,
- top contrasts,
- social-energy lens,
- emotional expression lens,
- decision and thinking lens.

Story rule:

Start with a recognizable contrast before showing the visual evidence.

Example flow:

> “A는 바로 말해야 마음이 정리되고, B는 정리된 뒤에야 말할 수 있습니다.”  
> then show expression-speed and conflict-style evidence.

Success test:

The user can explain one repeated misunderstanding in a kinder way.

---

## Module 3 — Relationship Flow: “This is the loop you keep entering”

Purpose:

- turn isolated incidents into a repeatable pattern,
- show how each person’s response triggers the other,
- reveal the relationship dynamic without assigning blame,
- surface useful and costly **unconscious roles** (initiator, stabilizer, pursuer, withdrawer, emotional tone-setter) without freezing them as permanent identity.

Primary assets:

- balance of power,
- expression speed,
- reassurance signal,
- recovery speed,
- residual,
- unconscious role play,
- closeness–distance rhythm.

Preferred presentation:

- loop diagram,
- sequence cards,
- before / during / after states,
- short narrative transitions.

Role rule:

Describe roles as current patterns with usefulness and cost—not as who is “stronger” or “more mature.”

Success test:

The user says:

> “아, 이게 우리 패턴이구나.”

---

## Module 4 — Hidden Heart: “What each person is really trying to receive”

Purpose:

- reveal the need beneath the behavior,
- increase compassion,
- separate intention from impact,
- connect reassurance, affection signals, expression timing, recovery, and residual emotion into recognizable misread patterns—not unrelated labels.

Primary assets:

- hidden heart,
- reassurance need,
- recognition need,
- vulnerability response,
- A→B gift,
- B→A gift.

Story rule:

Use contrast:

> “겉으로는 거리를 두는 것처럼 보여도, 실제로는 실망시키고 싶지 않아 정리할 시간을 찾는 경우가 있습니다.”

Success test:

The user sees the partner’s behavior differently than before.

---

## Module 5 — Why This Relationship Is Special

Purpose:

- identify the unique relational value,
- explain mutual activation,
- make the user feel the relationship has a distinct identity,
- provide emotional payoff after the heavier pattern analysis.

Primary assets:

- relationship-only dynamic,
- mutual growth,
- unique complement,
- defining paradox,
- “only with you” behavior,
- gift exchange.

Important:

This section must not promise destiny or permanence.

It should explain uniqueness, not superiority.

Success test:

The user understands why this relationship feels different without being told it is “meant to be.”

---

## Module 6 — Conflict Translation: “What you say vs what the other hears”

Purpose:

- make conflict highly recognizable,
- translate both sides,
- reveal escalation mechanics,
- create humor and relief where appropriate.

Primary assets:

- before / after dialogue,
- misheard meaning,
- trigger-response sequence,
- escalation points,
- repair entry points.

Recommended structure:

| What is said | What is meant | What the other hears | Better version |
|---|---|---|---|

Humor rule:

Humor may expose the pattern, but must never humiliate either person.

Success test:

The user wants to share the section with the partner.

---

## Module 7 — Do / Don’t: “Protect the relationship here”

Purpose:

- convert insight into clear behavior,
- distinguish person-specific guidance,
- reduce vague advice.

Primary assets:

- A should do,
- A should avoid,
- B should do,
- B should avoid,
- timing guidance,
- reassurance guidance,
- conflict pause guidance.

Prefer a limited set of high-value action types:

| Type | Product job |
|---|---|
| **Continue** | Protect a strength already helping the relationship |
| **Translate** | Explain one recurring difference more clearly |
| **Interrupt** | Stop one escalation loop |
| **Practice** | Build one new relational habit |
| **Discuss** | Have one necessary conversation |
| **Watch** | Notice one pressure point before it becomes structural |

Rule:

Every recommendation must connect to an earlier insight.

No advice may appear merely because it is generally healthy.

Actions must be specific enough to try, respectful to both people, realistic for ordinary life, and free of generic clichés.

Success test:

The user can choose one behavior to change immediately.

---

## Module 8 — Repair Guide: “What to do after the moment goes wrong”

Purpose:

- provide a practical repair sequence,
- account for different recovery speeds,
- avoid one-size-fits-all apology advice.

Primary assets:

- pause timing,
- who reaches first,
- what not to demand immediately,
- best reassurance form,
- restart sentence,
- repair ritual.

Example sequence:

1. Stop escalation.
2. Name what happened without proving who is right.
3. Match the slower person’s processing window.
4. Give the faster person a time anchor.
5. Reconnect with one clear sentence.

Success test:

The user could use the guide during a real conflict.

---

## Module 9 — Next Step: “Try this in real life”

Purpose:

- end with agency,
- prevent information overload,
- turn the report into a lived experiment.

Primary assets:

- one 24-hour action,
- one weekly habit,
- one sentence to try,
- one shared ritual,
- one question for each other.

Rule:

The final action set should be small enough to use.

Do not end with ten generic habits.

Success test:

The user takes or plans a real action.

---

## Module 10 — Relationship Horizon

Purpose:

- provide a future-facing synthesis,
- show how the relationship may mature,
- end with realistic hope.

Primary assets:

- timeline or evolution path,
- “when unexamined / when understood” contrast,
- future potential,
- closing relationship sentence.

May explore periods of easier connection, pressure or sensitivity, shifts in priorities, windows for repair or change, and themes that may grow more important over time.

Rule:

No deterministic predictions.

Use tendency language, not certainty language. Never claim that a breakup, marriage, betrayal, or specific event will happen.

Frame the future as a possibility shaped by awareness and action.

Success test:

The user leaves feeling clearer, not judged, trapped, or frightened.

---

## 7.1 Deep Read (depth layer, not a restart)

Deep Read rewards users who want richer understanding beneath the visible relationship.

It may deepen:

- individual relationship nature,
- hidden emotional needs,
- what each person brings to the other,
- what makes the bond feel special,
- deeper attraction and tension patterns,
- internal feelings that may not be openly expressed,
- long-form synthesis of the relationship.

**Product rule:** Deep Read must deepen the main report, not restart it. It must not repeat earlier cards in paragraph form. It should reveal connections that become meaningful only after the user understands the core structure.

---

## 7.2 Save, Share, and Revisit

Allow the report to become a relationship object rather than a one-time reading.

Potential experiences:

- a shareable relationship signature,
- saved insight cards,
- selected conversation prompts,
- partner-friendly summary,
- revisitable action plan,
- future comparison after retaking psychological input.

**Product rule:** Sharing must preserve dignity and nuance. Never encourage weaponizing a score, label, or insight against a partner.

This is a product surface around the ten modules—not a replacement narrative chapter system.

# 9. Storytelling Rules

## 9.1 The narrative unit

Every meaningful insight should follow this pattern:

1. **Scene or recognizable behavior**
2. **What it may mean for A**
3. **What it may mean for B**
4. **How the interaction forms**
5. **Evidence or visual confirmation**
6. **What changes the outcome**

Not every card needs all six visibly, but the product logic should contain them.

---

## 9.2 Scene before label

Bad:

> “A has high empathy and B has low structure.”

Better:

> “A는 대화 중 상대의 표정 변화를 먼저 읽는 편이고, B는 결론이 정리되어야 대화를 이어가기 쉽습니다.”

Then show the axis evidence.

---

## 9.3 Human language before system language

User-facing copy should prefer:

- “마음을 확인하는 속도” over internal enum labels,
- “다툰 뒤 다시 가까워지는 속도” over raw recovery field names,
- “말로 풀어야 하는 쪽 / 생각을 정리해야 하는 쪽” over abstract classifications.

System terminology may appear in expandable evidence details if useful.

---

## 9.4 No excerpt rewriting

LLM-generated narrative must not simply paraphrase one evidence snippet.

A strong insight should synthesize at least two meaningful sources whenever possible.

Examples:

- expression speed + conflict style,
- reassurance need + recovery speed,
- social energy + recognition need,
- day-stem tendency + 11-axis contrast,
- residual + role play.

---

## 9.5 Surprise without invention

The product should create non-obvious insights by combining real evidence, not by inventing hidden motives.

Valid surprise:

> “겉으로 먼저 다가가는 A가 실제로는 거절에 더 민감할 수 있습니다.”

Invalid surprise:

> “A는 어린 시절 버림받은 기억 때문에 집착합니다.”

unless such information is explicitly known and appropriate—which is outside the standard product scope.

---

## 9.6 One voice, multiple registers

The product has one consistent narrator:

- warm,
- perceptive,
- concise,
- emotionally intelligent,
- lightly playful when appropriate,
- never clinical,
- never mystical,
- never patronizing.

The register may shift:

- Hero: cinematic and clear,
- Difference Map: precise,
- Hidden Heart: tender,
- Conflict: direct and lightly humorous,
- Guide: practical,
- Closing: hopeful.

---

# 10. Copy Principles

## 10.1 Tone

The tone should feel like a very insightful friend who understands both people and is not trying to win the argument.

It should be:

- warm,
- elegant,
- intelligent,
- direct,
- specific,
- and humane.

---

## 10.2 Address law

Use names whenever possible.

Avoid anonymous “Person A / Person B” in narrative copy.

Avoid “너” except inside carefully constructed dialogue quotes.

For conflict scripts, use role-based labels when names would feel awkward:

- 먼저 말하고 싶은 쪽,
- 시간이 필요한 쪽,
- 화가 난 쪽,
- 상대,
- 먼저 다가가는 쪽.

---

## 10.3 Tendency, not verdict

Preferred:

- “~하는 편입니다”
- “~로 느낄 가능성이 있습니다”
- “압박이 커질수록 ~할 수 있습니다”
- “두 사람 사이에서는 ~로 나타나기 쉽습니다”

Avoid:

- “원래 이런 사람입니다”
- “반드시 이렇게 됩니다”
- “이 관계는 실패합니다”
- “절대 맞지 않습니다”

---

## 10.4 No generic praise symmetry

Do not force every section to flatter both people equally.

Balance does not mean false symmetry.

The goal is fair interpretation, not equal compliments.

---

## 10.5 Specificity test

Before approving copy, ask:

> Could this sentence apply unchanged to most couples?

If yes, it is too generic.

---

## 10.6 Humor rule

Humor is allowed when it creates recognition and lowers defensiveness.

Examples:

- lightly exposing a familiar conflict loop,
- showing how the same sentence is heard differently,
- revealing a timing mismatch.

Humor must never:

- mock vulnerability,
- shame either person,
- trivialize real pain,
- or become meme-first copy.

---

# 11. Visual Principles

## 11.1 Visual hierarchy

Meaning first, evidence second, detail third.

Each module should visually prioritize:

1. relationship insight,
2. user recognition,
3. evidence,
4. optional depth.

---

## 11.2 No dashboard-first design

The report must not look like an analytics dashboard.

Charts and tables are supporting instruments inside a story-led flow.

---

## 11.3 Use visual form by communication job

### Narrative card
Use for:

- one insight,
- emotional reframe,
- relationship signature.

### Comparison table
Use for:

- A/B contrast,
- fast translation,
- do / don’t.

### Radar
Use for:

- broad profile shape,
- similarity and gap overview.

### Loop diagram
Use for:

- repeating conflict or reassurance cycle.

### Timeline
Use for:

- maturation path,
- relationship evolution.

### Dialogue block
Use for:

- recognition,
- misinterpretation,
- repair language.

### Expandable deep read
Use for:

- nuance,
- evidence detail,
- longer explanation.

---

## 11.4 Density rhythm

The experience should alternate between:

- emotionally rich text,
- quick visual comprehension,
- high-recognition examples,
- and actionable summary.

Avoid long uninterrupted essay blocks.

Avoid endless card grids with no narrative transition.

---

## 11.5 Signature visual identity

The Hero may use a relationship motif or theme, but it must be abstract and relational—not a literal day-stem object metaphor.

The visual system should support emotional tone, not explain the analysis by itself.

# 12. Evidence Principles

## 12.1 Evidence source hierarchy

Potential evidence sources include:

- pair saju interactions,
- day-stem interaction,
- combined element notes,
- relationship signals,
- 11-axis psych data,
- relationship-specific derived axes,
- context outputs,
- resolver judgments,
- corroborating cross-hits,
- and structured pair dynamics.

The final technical mapping belongs in the Technical Blueprint.

This Product Blueprint defines only how evidence should behave in the experience.

---

## 12.2 Evidence tier

Every narrative claim should map to an evidence tier:

### Corroborated
Multiple sources point in the same direction.

Tone may be direct.

### Refined
The direction is supported, but nuance or conditions matter.

Tone should be specific and conditional.

### Tentative
Limited or mixed evidence.

Tone should clearly signal possibility.

### Unsupported
Do not show.

---

## 12.3 Severity is separate from confidence

A difficult pattern may be highly supported or only tentative.

A gentle difference may also be highly supported.

Do not confuse evidence strength with relational seriousness.

Recommended severity ladder:

- **S0 Strength** — supportive dynamic,
- **S1 Difference** — neutral contrast,
- **S2 Friction** — recurring misunderstanding,
- **S3 Strain** — repeated emotional cost,
- **S4 Structural Risk** — serious pattern requiring clear boundaries or external support.

The user-facing product should rarely expose these labels directly. They are editorial guidance.

---

# 12A. Premium Product Strategy

Premium must not mean “more text.”

## 12A.1 Free experience

The free Romantic experience should establish trust and feel useful—not intentionally broken.

It should answer:

- the overall relationship signature,
- primary strengths,
- one important difference,
- one meaningful interaction insight,
- a preview of what deeper analysis can reveal.

## 12A.2 Premium experience

Premium value comes from deeper synthesis, translation, and action:

- full relationship architecture across the ten modules,
- detailed difference map and role/loop dynamics,
- reassurance and affection interpretation,
- conflict loops and dialogue translation,
- personalized actions,
- deeper individual and bond interpretation (Deep Read),
- future perspective / horizon,
- saved or shareable relationship insights where productized.

### Premium promise

> Free tells users what the relationship feels like. Premium explains why it feels that way and what to do with that understanding.

## 12A.3 What must feel premium

- specificity,
- emotional accuracy,
- synthesis across multiple dimensions,
- balanced treatment of both people,
- useful visual explanation,
- realistic communication language,
- actionable guidance,
- elegant pacing,
- absence of generic filler.

Length alone is not premium. Complexity alone is not premium. Technical vocabulary is not premium.

# 13. Product Success Criteria

## 13.1 Memory outcomes

A world-class Romantic report should leave users remembering a few powerful truths—not twenty labels.

Ideal memory structure:

1. **Our relationship signature** — what kind of relationship this is  
2. **Our strongest connection** — what naturally brings us together  
3. **Our central misunderstanding** — where we repeatedly misread each other  
4. **Our relationship roles / loop** — what each person tends to carry or enact  
5. **Our next useful step** — what would make the relationship work better  
6. **Our future watchpoint** — what deserves attention as the bond develops  

If users remember many labels but cannot explain these, the product has failed.

## 13.2 User success

The report succeeds when users can answer:

- What kind of relationship do we have?
- What brings us together?
- Where do we misunderstand each other?
- What roles or loops do we tend to enter?
- What happens when we fight?
- What can we do differently?
- What should we pay attention to over time?

Behavioral signals of success include:

- recognize themselves immediately,
- share a section with their partner,
- understand a recurring conflict differently,
- feel less blamed or ashamed,
- see the partner more compassionately,
- use a suggested sentence,
- revisit the guide after conflict,
- take one action,
- or want to explore another relationship product.

Completion rate alone is not the definition of success.

## 13.3 Experience success

- The central relationship pattern is recognizable within the first mobile scroll.
- Early modules provide value without technical knowledge.
- Every major visual answers a specific user question.
- Every major friction pattern includes explanation before advice.
- Similarity, compatibility, and quality are never treated as the same concept.
- Attraction and compatibility remain distinct.
- Both people retain dignity throughout.
- Deep Read adds meaning rather than repetition.
- Future interpretation uses uncertainty responsibly.
- Unsupported topics are not presented as authoritative scores.
- The report feels like one coherent product, not a pile of generated modules.
- Premium value is obvious without artificial withholding.
- The experience remains elegant and clear on mobile.

## 13.4 Business success

- Free users understand the product’s unique value.
- Premium value is visible through depth, synthesis, and action rather than word count.
- The report creates natural save, share, and revisit behavior.
- The Romantic structure can inform Friend, Work, and Family blueprints without forcing those products into Romantic logic.
- Product meaning remains stable even if implementation changes.

---

## 13.5 Module-level quality checks

Every major module must pass:

### Recognition check
Does it contain a concrete, familiar scene or pattern?

### Specificity check
Could this apply unchanged to most couples?

### Evidence check
Is the claim supported by the available sources?

### Fairness check
Does it explain both sides without false equivalence?

### Freshness check
Does it add a new realization?

### Action check
Does the user know what to do with the insight?

### Tone check
Does it sound warm, intelligent, and non-judgmental?

# 14. Do / Don’t Constitution

## Do

- lead with human meaning,
- use names,
- show scenes,
- synthesize multiple evidence sources,
- preserve useful existing assets,
- create clear transitions,
- explain both intention and impact,
- use visuals for comprehension,
- make difficult truths actionable,
- and end with believable hope.

## Don’t

- lead with a grade,
- rank relationships,
- use “soulmate” certainty,
- use shallow elemental metaphors,
- expose raw backend labels as the main experience,
- repeat the same insight across cards,
- invent trauma or motives,
- give generic advice unrelated to evidence,
- bury the user in charts,
- or confuse Romantic with Marriage.

# 15. Product Decisions Locked by This Document

The following decisions are considered locked unless a future product decision explicitly supersedes them:

1. Romantic is a story-led relationship experience, not an analysis report.
2. Grade, score-led identity, and compatibility ranking are removed from the core experience.
3. Existing successful assets are preserved and repositioned rather than redesigned without reason.
4. The relationship—not either individual—is the protagonist.
5. The five human questions remain the stable content foundation.
6. The user journey follows recognition → understanding → action → hope.
7. 11-axis radar and comparison tables remain evidence assets, not primary story drivers.
8. Before / After dialogue, Hidden Heart, Gift, Guide, Difference Map, and Timeline remain core product assets.
9. Day-stem and saju concepts may inform concise behavioral interpretation, but not decorative literal metaphors.
10. Romantic and Marriage remain separate products with clear ownership boundaries.
11. Every major insight must be evidence-aware and confidence-aware.
12. Every difficult section must contain a useful path forward.
13. This document (`05A`) is the sole Romantic Product SSOT; former `05_Romantic_Product_Blueprint.md` is absorbed and deleted.
14. Free vs Premium is differentiated by synthesis and action depth—not by word count or artificial breakage of free.
15. Attraction and compatibility must remain distinct.

# 17. Future Expansion Rules

Future features may be added only when they satisfy at least one of the five core human questions and improve the emotional journey.

A new feature must answer:

1. What user question does this answer?
2. What new realization does it create?
3. What evidence supports it?
4. Where does it belong in the narrative journey?
5. Does it duplicate an existing asset?
6. Is it Romantic or Marriage?
7. What action or reframe does it enable?

Features that cannot answer these questions should not be added.

# 18. Product Review Checklist

Before approving a Romantic experience, ask:

- Does the Hero work without a grade?
- Is the relationship signature specific and repeatable?
- Does the story begin before the charts?
- Can the user recognize at least one real-life scene early?
- Are the 11 axes interpreted rather than dumped?
- Does the report explain the relationship loop?
- Does Hidden Heart create a genuine reframe?
- Is “why special” unique without destiny claims?
- Does the conflict section translate both sides?
- Are do / don’t recommendations evidence-linked?
- Is the repair guide usable during a real conflict?
- Is the final action small enough to try?
- Does the report end with hope, not evaluation?
- Is anything in the product actually Marriage content?
- Is any copy generic, mystical, repetitive, or unsupported?

If any answer is no, the experience is not ready.

# 20. Closing Principle

The standard is not:

> “Did we show the analysis?”

The standard is:

> “Did the user understand their relationship in a way they could feel, remember, and use?”

Ahaitsme Romantic is complete only when the user can leave with a clearer story about the relationship—and a better next move inside it.

Users should be able to say not only “This is accurate,” but:

> “Now I understand what happens between us, and I know what we can do next.”
